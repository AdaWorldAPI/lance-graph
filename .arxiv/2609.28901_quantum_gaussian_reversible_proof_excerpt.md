# Quantum Gaussian circuit: the reversible-history analogy

**Source:** Hochang Lee, Kyung Chul Jeong, Panjin Kim, *A Quantum Circuit for Gaussian Elimination*, [arXiv:2609.28901v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28901), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28901v1); 2026-09-24. Abstract, §§1, 2, 4.  
**Research status (2026-10-21):** Primary-source excerpt. **Quantum speedup of Lance-graph is not claimed.**

## Source-grounded excerpt

The authors give a **reversible quantum circuit** for Gaussian elimination over arbitrary finite fields (generalizing prior GF(2) work). Their pseudo-row-echelon representation preserves sufficient information to make the normally many-to-one elimination transformation reversible. They analyze Toffoli gate count/depth and garbage/supplemental space. One reported design uses no extra **persistent garbage** beyond input storage; this does not mean there are no temporary qubits, classical pointers, or cheap quantum hardware for QR256.

The paper explicitly distinguishes a reversible state transform from a purely lossy row echelon reduction, since an irreversible transform cannot be inverted from its output alone.

## Hypothetical transfer

For **classical**, finite, deterministic QR transitions, borrow the principle: save a minimal reversible operation schedule or enough before/delta evidence to **verify** the transition and optionally undo it, while keeping read-only SoA buffers untouched. Writeback remains a separately acknowledged transaction. Mathematical equivalence of expressions alone does not certify an external database commit.

## Falsifiers / test path Q

- **Q1, reverse schedule:** GF(2) elementary XOR and row-swap steps replay forward and backward to the exact original bit matrix. Record pivot choices and versioned initial state; compare full 8 KiB.
- **Q2, lossy step:** Pure `OR`, projection and existential folding are many-to-one. A log that contains only final QR plus an operation name must **fail** reversal. Add BEFORE+delta or another adequate witness and verify rollback.
- **Q3, resource accounting:** Compare full snapshots, XOR dirty deltas, hash-consed proof DAG, and operation schedule. Report bytes, cold verification time, inverse replay time and refusal rate.
- **Q4, writeback separation:** An unchanged source generation permits verified computation; changing source generation before commit must refuse or revalidate. Simulate failure between prepare, sidecar approval and write acknowledgement.
- **Silence twin:** No-op transition emits no actual dirty bits and never logs a successful data-store commit.

**Verdict:** Research transfer is *replay/information preservation*, not a quantum execution backend.
