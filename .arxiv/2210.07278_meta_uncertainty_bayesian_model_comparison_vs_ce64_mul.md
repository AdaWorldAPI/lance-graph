## Source-derived claim

Bayesian model comparison gives posterior model probabilities (PMPs) `π_j = p(M_j | y)` over a **specified candidate model set**. A PMP close to 1 is not by itself a guarantee that repeating the experiment will lead to the same model preference. The authors introduce a distribution *over possible future PMP vectors* by simulating data under candidate models, fitting meta-models to the model-implied PMP distributions, and combining their posterior predictives into a data-informed mixture. Its purpose is to assess replicability and mitigate spuriously strong model preference under the assumptions of the candidate models.

The paper distinguishes **three levels**, not three grades of subjective confidence:

1. **Level 1:** uncertainty in the PMP vector for a finite observed dataset.
2. **Level 2:** variability of model-implied PMP vectors across replicated/simulated datasets under the same generating model.
3. **Level 3:** uncertainty about the fitted meta-model's distribution of PMPs, owing to a finite number of simulations `K`.

The key construction in §3.3, Eq. (8), is a posterior predictive **mixture distribution for future PMP vectors**, weighted by observed PMPs; it is not simply a new direct posterior for model truth. Proposition 2 states non-vanishing predictive variance under the stated model-mixture conditions even when observed PMPs concentrate on one model.

**Source example (§3.5):** For an atypical sample in a two-model Beta–Binomial comparison, an observed PMP is roughly `0.999`; the paper reports a predictive-mixture **mean** of roughly `0.55`. This is an illustration of model-comparison instability, **not** a universal confidence correction or a claim that `p(M_1|y)=0.55`.

**Caveat:** Closed candidate sets and prior-predictive simulation can be misspecified. The paper's guarantees do not prove that every true explanation lies in the modeled family or that simulations match real experience.

## Potential research transfer (HYPOTHESIS, NOT THE PAPER'S RESULT)

An **offline, versioned metacognitive evaluation** can compare a finite set of rival inference policies, reasoning styles or causal models, subject to a declared likelihood/generative model. Store a separate audit receipt keyed by a stable thought version, model-family version and independently grounded data provenance:

- Observed distribution of support for competing models or strategies.
- Distribution of outputs expected from replication on synthetic/held-out cases.
- Simulation/sample counts and uncertainty in the replication estimate.
- Agreement/disagreement with independent outcomes; out-of-family discrepancy gate.
- Choice of next observation or abstention, with explicit limitations.

Do **not** treat 64 thought slots as independent evidence, the CE64 `Certification3` ordinal as a probability, or duplicated Narsese/MeTTa/Datalog derivations as repeated independent model observations.

**First falsification probe:** A model reports near-certain preference in one small dataset, but repeated held-out datasets reverse it. Compare baseline certainty-only selection against explicit predictive-variability assessment at matched compute. Include (a) identical duplicated evidence, (b) an out-of-model generating process, (c) large-sample convergence, and (d) variance due only to estimator simulation count. A good guard must expose instability without claiming the candidate set is complete.
