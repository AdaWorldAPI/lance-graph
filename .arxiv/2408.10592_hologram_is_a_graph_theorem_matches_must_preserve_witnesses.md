## Punchline

> **Keep observations addressable; let theorems be independently declared consumers of the same graph. A fast candidate mask is not a proof: pattern binding and its constraints must still pass.**

This paper's word *hologram* denotes a **heterogeneous attributed geometry graph**, NOT HRR/FHRR, a phase-bound VSA, or distributed bit-vector superposition. Its real contribution is to make a collection of theorem templates reusable over a shared graph representation and to improve the *choice* of which template to attempt.

Portable separation:

~~~text
text + diagram
     ↓ parse (may be wrong)
graph G = primitives + relations + mathematical and visual attributes
     ↓ select theorem candidate (policy is a heuristic)
match pattern under structural constraints
     ↓ enforce mathematical and visual constraints
       ├─ proving model: derive relationship ΔG
       └─ property model: derive equations E
     ↓ solve equations / update derivation state
explicit theorem + operands + relation + equation + answer
~~~

No unvalidated visual resemblance may silently become a mathematical theorem.

## Paper extraction: concrete mechanism

### H1. One global geometry graph with distinct attribute kinds (paper §Method / Hologram; PDF p. 3)

Paper notation: \`G=(V,E,A,Â)\`.

- \`V\`: points, lines, angles, arcs, circles, polygons.
- \`E\`: geometric relations (incidence, adjacency, perpendicularity, parallelism, congruence, similarity). HGR uses an **undirected** graph for these relations.
- \`A\`: measured / mathematical values including lengths, angles, areas and target attribute \`τ\`.
- \`Â\`: diagram-derived visual cues such as approximate positions, apparent lengths and apparent angles. The paper explicitly excludes these from formal calculation; they help disambiguate candidate matches.

The same \`G\` is read by distinct operations; the distinction between admissible mathematical facts and visual hints is a useful cross-consumer boundary.

### H2. Theorem templates are first-class models (paper §Graph Model Construction; PDF pp. 4–5)

There are two kinds of templates:

~~~text
proving model  M_prov = (G_p, R, Ĉ, C, ΔG)
property model M_prop = (G_p, R, Ĉ, E)
~~~

- \`G_p\` is an attributed-agnostic *pattern graph* (for structural matching).
- \`R\` is a readable relation template for the resulting explanation, not a freely inferred fact.
- \`Ĉ\` denotes visual constraints used to resolve potential pattern ambiguity.
- \`C\` denotes mathematical preconditions of a proving model.
- \`ΔG\` introduces derived vertices, edges or attributes after proof.
- \`E\` denotes equations a property model can generate.

Proving a relationship and using an already-established relationship to form an equation are different operations. HGR's example first proves parallelism, then applies the parallelogram property.

### H3. Structural matching is VF3, not a mere bitmap intersection (paper §Model Matching; PDF p. 5)

Paper matcher seeks a mapping \`M: V_p → V_g\` preserving the graph's required structure and edge/non-edge relationships; the implementation uses VF3.

After finding a mapping, HGR evaluates each mathematical constraint against the mapped primitives, then checks auxiliary visual constraints. Only successfully matched models may produce their outcomes.

**Substrate distinction:** \`R = P_a & P_b & aperture\` can prune candidate addresses. It cannot *alone* certify a multi-vertex graph match, variable binding, required edge presence/absence, or numeric constraints. Any RelAlg / Datalog / Quack / R2IL lowering must preserve all of these.

### H4. Iterate derivation, not just retrieval (paper §Reasoner; PDF pp. 5–6)

A successful proving model produces \`G' = G + ΔG(M(V_p))\`. A successful property model adds instantiated equations to \`E_total\`. The solver evaluates \`E_total\`, updates available mathematical attributes, and repeats until target \`τ\` is reached.

Graph Transformer embeddings encode current graph state for a DQN theorem-choice policy; they do not replace VF3 matching or numerical theorem checks. Learned scheduling and mathematical validity are different layers.

### H5. Study scale and measured claims (paper §Implementation / §Evaluation; PDF pp. 6–7)

- Geometry3K has **3,002** multiple-choice geometry problems, with text, diagram and parsing annotations.
- Model pool: **13 proving** plus **51 property** models (64 total).
- The paper's accuracy metric chooses the nearest multiple-choice answer to the solver's numeric output; when no output is produced, a random choice is made. Thus it is not a formal-proof-success rate.
- HGR overall accuracy: **68.7%** using its parser; **89.6%** using ground-truth (GT) parsing. Do not report 89.6% as end-to-end.
- With GT: GeoDRL baseline **89.4%**, Inter-GPS **78.3%**.
- With GT: HGR averages **2.26 theorem applications**, **7.29 relations**, **17.55 equations** per solved problem (Table 2).
- HGR uses a Graph Transformer + DQN theorem selector, pre-trained with heuristic/random selection samples. These components optimize search order, not the semantics of rules.

Ablation with GT parsing (paper Table 3, PDF p. 7):

| Configuration | Overall accuracy |
|---|---:|
| HGR complete | 89.6% |
| Without theorem-selection agent | 83.4% |
| Without proving models | 79.5% |
| Without property models | **27.0%** |
| Without mathematical constraints \`C\` | 73.6% |
| Without visual constraints \`Ĉ\` | 63.1% |

**Interpretation:** A theorem pool, formal applicability constraints, and rules that produce equations are consequential. The strongest ablation is property models, but percentages alone do not identify any improvement in the proposed lance-graph executor.

### H6. Known boundaries (paper §Failure Case; PDF p. 8)

The published system fails on tasks requiring auxiliary geometric constructions and some shaded-region area calculations. Finite prepared-theorem coverage is a limitation. A new theorem template is not equivalent to the ability to invent new mathematics.

## Extraction → lance-graph design (our engineering hypotheses, NOT paper claims)

### L1. Reuse a typed theorem consumer over canonical graph observations

~~~text
Observed geometry (immutable, source-positioned)
    ↓ ClassView / typed primitive and relation reading
Candidate theorem
    ↓ symbolic simplification and presence masks
Bound graph match
    ↓ structural + numerical preconditions
Derived theorem witness + equations
    ↓ separate derived generation / virtual branch
Replayable proof / answer
~~~

Suggested logical representation; do not introduce new stored structs until a falsifier proves existing ClassView/SoA/Quack machinery insufficient:

~~~text
TheoremTemplate {
    pattern: typed relation predicates,
    required_edges: declared,
    prohibited_edges: declared,
    math_checks: exact or tolerance-typed predicates,
    visual_hints: candidate-ranking ONLY,
    conclusion: derivation recipe,
    explanation: operand-bound template
}
~~~

The source paper does not propose HHTL, SoA, masks, CE64, NAL or R2IL.

### L2. Visual/heuristic evidence cannot certify the theorem

\`Â\` can prioritize a match, but a proof certificate must be based on authorized structural/mathematical conditions. Example: two diagram lines *looking* parallel does not establish parallelism.

This is analogous to \`Primed != Attended != Proven\`, but HGR itself does not define those categories.

### L3. Preserve derivation provenance when updating a graph

The paper updates its problem-local \`G\` in place. A replayable substrate can instead retain:

~~~text
input_version
theorem_model_id + theorem_model_version
bound_vertex_addresses / role assignments
source relation witnesses
structural and mathematical check results
derived_relation_or_equation
output_generation
~~~

The expansion \`ΔG\` should be a **derived view or explicitly committed version**, never indistinguishable from the original diagram observations.

### L4. Theorem selector is not the theorem executor

For a fair comparison: hold exactly the same 64 models, theorem constraints, equation solver and parse output fixed; change *only* the candidate selection / execution path. Compare a heuristic model selector, HGR DQN (if reproducible), and optional information-gain scheduling separately.

### L5. R2IL lowering has to preserve joins and graph isomorphism checks

Mask-first candidate pruning may reduce loads; it is not a proof of VF3 parity. Multi-entity binding, edge labels, required absences, symmetry and numerical checks all need independently executable oracles.

No claim of O(1) end-to-end VF3 elimination, no claimed speedup, and no speculative benchmark number.
