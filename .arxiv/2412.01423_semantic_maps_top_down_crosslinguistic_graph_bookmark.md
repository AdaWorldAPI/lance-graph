## Source abstract

Classical semantic map models represent conceptual relatedness across languages under a **connectivity hypothesis**. The authors propose constructing conceptual spaces from a dense graph and then pruning to **maximum spanning trees**, with intrinsic/extrinsic evaluation criteria that balance structural coverage and precision. Their case study concerns crosslinguistic supplementary adverbs.

The authors position the method against manually constructed semantic maps. This is not a general formal proof that all meanings in natural language are tree-shaped, nor proof that a tree entails correct grammatical inference.

## Future Haspelmath / DeepNSM-v2 relevance (hypotheses)

- Examine whether semantic-map connectivity can provide a **candidate-space constraint** for independently attested role/semantic relations across languages.
- Keep language-specific readings, referential/anaphoric uncertainty, and attestation separate from shared canonical claim identity.
- A spanning tree can lose alternative paths or polysemy; preserve such alternatives before any graph reduction.
- Test multiple grammatical orientations over a four-sentence anaphoric ring against the **same justified canonical conclusions**, not merely aesthetically similar layouts.
- Do not confuse the paper's crosslinguistic semantic maps with an executable RelAlg/Datalog AST, CE64 role, Haspelmath grammar ABI, or a verified semantic crosswalk.
