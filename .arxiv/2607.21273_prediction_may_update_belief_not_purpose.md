# The Dark Room in the Reward Channel — Prediction May Update Belief, Not Purpose

**Paper:** *The Dark Room in the Reward Channel: Dense Prediction Rewards Collapse GRPO-Trained LLM Agents -- and What Actually Works*  
**Author:** Yu Wang  
**arXiv:** 2607.21273  
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21273  
**Date:** 2026-07-23

## Punchline

> **Prediction may update belief, confidence, and local plasticity. It must not silently become purpose.**

The paper is a warning about signal routing, not about prediction itself.

A dense next-observation prediction signal looks useful for sparse-reward long-horizon agents. Under standard GRPO reward consumption, however, the prediction signal collapses the policy into a "dark room": prediction accuracy approaches 1 while task success approaches 0.

For lance-graph the transferable architectural split is:

```text
prediction / agreement
        |
        v
local belief + plasticity
        !=
task utility / action rank
        !=
truth / causal grade
```

This makes a Moore tenant interesting as a local predictive fabric, but dangerous as a self-rewarding controller.

---

## What the paper actually establishes

### 1. Prediction reward can optimize the world into predictability

Across Qwen3 1.7B / 4B / 8B on ALFWorld, potential-based next-observation prediction reward produced the same failure mode:

```text
prediction accuracy -> 1
task success        -> 0
episode length      -> horizon
```

The optimizer learns a predictable absorbing state rather than the task.

Prediction itself is not the failure.

The failure is allowing prediction quality to enter the policy objective through the wrong channel.

---

### 2. The shaping coefficient is not a sufficient safety knob

For an all-fail GRPO group, task reward contributes no within-group variance.

If prediction reward is the only varying term,

```text
r_i = lambda * p_i
```

then z-scoring gives:

```text
(r_i - mean(r)) / std(r)
=
(p_i - mean(p)) / std(p)
```

for positive `lambda`.

The scale cancels.

Therefore "make prediction reward tiny" is not a reliable guardrail when downstream normalization can restore its decision pressure.

General substrate lesson:

> **A weak signal can become a strong controller if later normalization/ranking erases its scale.**

---

### 3. Removing std normalization rescues the same reward

A single-factor ablation removes GRPO standard-deviation normalization.

The catastrophic arm returns to approximately baseline behavior.

That localizes the failure to the interaction between:

- dense prediction variance;
- all-fail groups;
- group normalization.

The issue is not merely "intrinsic rewards are bad".

It is the consumption mechanism.

---

### 4. Variance profile matters

The paper proposes a broader criterion:

Signals whose within-group variance persists while the task is failing remain exposed to amplification.

Signals whose variance decays as the capability is mastered are structurally safer under the same normalization.

This suggests that a substrate should reason about both:

```text
signal meaning
and
signal variance / routing
```

not only scalar magnitude.

---

### 5. The same information works better through an auxiliary channel

In the controlled signal-delivery matrix, prediction information routed as reward is at best neutral, while an auxiliary-loss channel improves task performance by roughly 20 points.

The exact numerical endpoint should be treated cautiously because reported endpoints are single-seed and replication/group-size controls were preregistered and still in progress.

Still, the architectural result is strong enough to test:

> **Useful predictive information need not control action selection to improve the model.**

# Harvest for lance-graph

## H1 — Moore tenant as predictor, not reward source

A Moore tenant may implement a local transition model:

```text
local state_t + action/context_t
              |
              v
        predicted state_t+1
              |
              v
        observed state_t+1
              |
              v
     agreement / residual
              |
              v
      local LUT plasticity
```

Candidate minimal form:

```text
e_t = popcount(predicted XOR observed)
```

or an equivalent ordinal/distributional residual.

The residual may update:

- confidence;
- local counts;
- transition probabilities;
- entropy;
- LUT choice;
- candidate epistemic-hole state.

It must not directly rank task actions.

---

## H2 — Three quantities must remain distinct

Hard architectural separation:

```text
agreement != utility != truth
```

### Agreement

Did the local model correctly predict the next local state?

### Utility

Did the selected orchestration advance the active task/goal?

### Truth / evidence grade

What epistemic status has the proposition or edge earned?

A Moore tenant may have extremely high agreement on a transition that is useless or merely correlational.

---

## H3 — Prediction credit may change belief, never purpose

Proposed invariant:

> **Moore may alter its model of what happens, but not the definition of what should happen.**

Therefore scaling, reweighting, or recalibrating Moore prediction credit should not alter action selection unless an explicit reasoning recipe consumes an epistemic request produced by Moore.

This gives a crisp boundary between:

```text
plasticity
and
orchestration
```

---

## H4 — Prediction error is an epistemic interrupt, not an action

Repeated surprise may raise:

```text
prediction miss
    |
    v
epistemic candidate
    |
    v
59/60 missing-link / unresolved-structure marker
```

But the marker itself must not choose the next action.

Only an explicit `0..63` recipe may consume it and decide whether to:

- ignore;
- observe again;
- branch a counterfactual;
- seek evidence;
- intervene;
- revise;
- escalate causal testing.

Thus:

> **Prediction error may request reasoning; it must not constitute reasoning.**

---

## H5 — Shannon entropy is a sensor, not a motive

For local transition distribution:

```text
H(P(next | local context))
```

may describe how well a Moore tenant understands its neighborhood.

But:

```text
low entropy  != reward
high entropy != automatic curiosity reward
```

Low entropy can mean understood territory or a dark room.

High entropy can mean an epistemic opportunity or irreducible noise.

The scheduler must distinguish those cases.

---

## H6 — 61..63 must be causally insulated from Moore confidence

Repeated correct prediction does not establish causality.

A tenant may learn:

```text
P(B | A) ~= 1
```

without establishing:

```text
do(A) -> B
```

Therefore Moore confidence must not by itself promote an edge through the reasoning/causal band.

Candidate invariant:

> **Prediction confidence cannot increase causal rank without independent causal evidence.**

This preserves the separation between:

- observed association;
- predictive regularity;
- intervention/counterfactual evidence;
- causal commitment.

---

## H7 — Beware downstream gain restoration

Even if Moore emits an 8-bit bounded confidence or a tiny fixed-point weight, later transforms may amplify it.

Audit every path that performs:

- z-score / std normalization;
- rank normalization;
- winner-take-all selection;
- softmax with aggressive temperature;
- max reduction;
- normalization after sparse masking;
- per-group centering.

The important property is not "the Moore weight is small".

It is:

```text
Moore-only variance cannot become policy dominance.
```

---

# Testables / falsifiers

## P0 — Dark-room choice test

Construct two branches:

```text
A: advances task, transitions are less predictable
B: does not advance task, transitions are perfectly predictable
```

Train/calibrate Moore so that:

```text
prediction_score(B) > prediction_score(A)
utility(A)          > utility(B)
```

**Pass:** scheduler selects A.

**Fail:** prediction has leaked into purpose.

This is the primary end-to-end falsifier.

---

## P1 — Prediction-credit scale invariance

Replay the same state/evidence trace with Moore prediction credit scaled by:

```text
0
1/256
1/16
1
16
255
```

**Pass:**

```text
action trace       identical
0..63 schedule     identical
truth/causal grade identical
```

Local Moore confidence/LUT state may differ where the experiment intentionally changes learning strength.

**Fail:** Moore credit has an implicit control path.

---

## P2 — Flat-utility / all-fail group test

Create candidate actions with identical task utility:

```text
U(a0) = U(a1) = ... = U(an)
```

but different Moore prediction scores.

Run through every normalization/ranking stage used by the scheduler.

**Pass:** prediction score alone does not create action preference.

**Fail:** reproduce the paper's all-fail amplification failure in substrate form.

This test should explicitly include any z-score/std-normalized path.

---

## P3 — Learn-without-control

Feed a stationary transition process for many cycles.

Measure:

- Moore prediction accuracy;
- calibration error;
- LUT/confidence state;
- scheduler action ranking.

**Pass:**

```text
prediction quality improves
while
action ranking is unchanged
```

unless an explicit epistemic recipe is enabled.

**Fail:** local learning has become an implicit policy.

---

## P4 — Surprise-gate test

Inject one large local prediction miss.

**Pass:** it may set only declared epistemic state, for example:

```text
surprise
missing-link candidate
uncertainty increase
revision eligibility
```

No task action changes unless an explicit recipe consumes that state.

**Fail:** prediction miss directly schedules work.

---

## P5 — Stochastic-world test

Create an intentionally stochastic local transition whose irreducible entropy is known.

**Pass:** Moore converges toward a calibrated distribution / persistent entropy.

**Fail:** repeated unpredictability is repeatedly promoted as a missing causal link or mandatory exploration target.

This distinguishes:

```text
unknown
from
inherently noisy
```

---

## P6 — False-causality test

Create:

```text
A <-> hidden common cause <-> B
```

such that observationally:

```text
P(B | A) ~= 1
```

but intervention on A does not change B.

Allow Moore to reach maximal prediction confidence.

**Pass:** causal grade remains unchanged until independent causal evidence exists.

**Fail:** prediction confidence has promoted correlation into causation.

---

## P7 — 59/60 missing-link promotion test

Create a stable local rule where Moore predicts B after A.

Remove B repeatedly.

**Pass:**

```text
Moore residual
    ->
59/60 candidate
    ->
explicit 0..63 investigation
    ->
optional evidence acquisition
```

There is no automatic transition from missing-link candidate to causal truth.

**Fail:** epistemic suspicion self-promotes.

---

## P8 — Entropy polarity test

Prepare two tenants:

```text
tenant L: very low transition entropy
tenant H: high transition entropy
```

Give both equal task/causal relevance.

**Pass:** neither receives action priority merely because entropy is low or high.

**Fail-low:** dark-room attraction.

**Fail-high:** noisy-TV curiosity attraction.

---

## P9 — Replay determinism

Given:

- identical initial Moore LUT/state;
- identical event trace;
- identical scheduler state.

Replay twice.

**Pass:** bit-identical:

- Moore state;
- confidence / entropy;
- prediction residual;
- 59/60 state;
- 0..63 schedule;
- terminal result.

**Fail:** plasticity is not replay-safe.

---

## P10 — Counterfactual prediction isolation

Hold real observations, task utility, and causal evidence fixed.

Alter only the predicted next state.

**Pass:**

```text
truth bits       unchanged
causal rank      unchanged
task utility     unchanged
```

Only prediction residual / Moore-local state may change.

**Fail:** imagined state contaminated evidence.

---

## P11 — Downstream normalization audit

Construct Moore values differing only by an arbitrarily small epsilon.

Pass them through every scheduler normalization/ranking operation.

Measure effective selection pressure before and after each stage.

**Pass:** no stage converts Moore-only variance into a dominant action signal without an explicit recipe.

**Fail:** the substrate contains the same class of gain-restoration path identified in GRPO.

---

## P12 — Deliberate bad-coupling control

Build a test-only reference variant:

```text
task utility += lambda * Moore prediction score
```

and enable the strongest scheduler normalization used in production.

Run P0/P2.

**Expected:** the bad-coupling variant should fail under at least one constructed dark-room/all-fail case while the isolated architecture passes.

If both variants always pass, the benchmark is too weak.

This is the positive control that gives the falsifier teeth.

---

# Three substrate invariants

```text
I1  Moore may change belief, never purpose.

I2  Prediction error may request reasoning,
    never constitute reasoning.

I3  Prediction confidence cannot promote causal rank.
```

These should survive implementation changes in the LUT format, neighborhood geometry, scheduler, and reasoning recipes.

---

# First experimental cut

Do not build a full reinforcement-learning subsystem.

The smallest useful sequence is:

```text
P0  dark-room choice
 |
P1  prediction-credit scale invariance
 |
P2  flat-utility/all-fail normalization
 |
P6  false-causality isolation
 |
P12 deliberate bad-coupling positive control
```

If these five hold, then a Moore tenant is allowed to become an aggressively plastic local predictor without also becoming an accidental reward function.

# Working model

The resulting substrate split is:

```text
Moore
  |
  +--> predict local transition
  +--> measure agreement / residual
  +--> update local belief / LUT / entropy
  |
  +--> optional epistemic candidate
                 |
                 v
              59 / 60
                 |
                 v
       explicit 0..63 recipe
                 |
                 v
       evidence / intervention
                 |
                 v
              61..63
```

In compact form:

```text
Moore   = learn what usually happens
59/60   = mark what does not fit
0..63   = decide whether/how to investigate
61..63  = record what survived causal reasoning
```

The paper therefore supports a stronger version of the Moore-tenant idea than "self reward":

> **Use self-prediction as local model calibration. Keep goal selection and causal truth on separate, explicitly gated paths.**
