# Metacognitive architectures — The missing object is a replayable, actionable self-model

**Paper:** Robin Nolte, Mihai Pomarlan, Ayden Janssen, Daniel Beßler, Kamyar Javanmardi, Sascha Jongebloed, Robert Porzel, John Bateman, Michael Beetz, Rainer Malaka, *How Metacognitive Architectures Remember Their Own Thoughts: A Systematic Review*.  
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13467 (v1 February 2025; v2 July 2025). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13467 (75 pages).  
**Peer-reviewed version:** *How metacognitive architectures remember their own thoughts: a systematic scoping review*, *Artificial Intelligence Review* **59**, article 213, published 27 August 2026, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10462-026-11669-5.  
**Supplementary data:** https://github.com/mrnolte/cma_review_data ; project visualizations https://dml.uni-bremen.de/ease/review/ .  
**Source level:** primary source arXiv preprint read through all thematic sections, cross-checked against newer journal text; differences in *title, publication status and scope wording* are explicit. Numerical observations below refer to the studied corpus, **not all cognitive architectures**.  
**Distillation date:** 2026-10-11.

## Punchline

> **Episodic logs become metacognition only when the system can retrieve a faithful representation of its own prior processing, diagnose it, and change subsequent processing on the basis of that diagnosis. The field has architectures, but little controlled evidence that their metacognitive memories are responsible for improvement.**

For lance-graph: split-tunnel + Witness + recipe receipts can give an audit-quality *substrate* for this, but **durable trace ≠ self-model ≠ correction ≠ self-control**. Build the smallest circuit from failed reasoning through independently checkable diagnosis into a bounded correction, then ablate the link.

# Part I — What the authors actually studied

## 1. Research question, boundary and evidence level

Central question: **How do models of cognition model their own cognition?** More specifically, how do Computational Metacognitive Architectures (CMAs) represent, store, retrieve and use *episodic metacognitive experiences*?

The study is an **explorative systematic scoping review**, not a benchmark competition or meta-analysis establishing a winning architecture.

- Search: **16 databases**, consulted **December 2023 – June 2024**.
- Included: **101 reports**, describing **35 distinct CMAs**.
- Extraction: **20-item framework**, organized into context, data content/structure, and objectives/evaluation.
- Primary corpus largely predates the 2025/2026 wave of LLM agents; do not project the frequencies into 2026 deployed agents.
- The review was not prospectively registered and did not have a preregistered protocol (the journal version says this explicitly).
- Only **about 17% of the 35 architectures** had a quantitative evaluation relevant to the review's specific technique.
- Authors flag terminology fragmentation, missing technical detail, limited standardization, selective/uneven reports, restricted toy-domain evaluation and weak head-to-head comparison.

This 17% is **not** the proportion of papers with any numerical results, nor evidence that the remaining 83% cannot perform metacognition. It describes relevant quantitative ablation evidence.

## 2. Definition ladder: cognition -> metacognition -> experience -> episodic memory

The journal text formalizes a useful three-part hierarchy (Methods §2.1.1–2.1.2):

**Model-based cognition:** a system uses models of other systems when interacting with them.

**Model-based metacognition:** a cognitive system uses **self-models** in interacting with itself. This is an operational criterion, not a claim that the computer has phenomenal consciousness.

**Metacognitive experience:** an internal model of the system's own processes/states that it can deliberately access and inspect.

The review follows the *representational/access* sense of consciousness, explicitly separating it from phenomenal subjectivity. It focuses on traces with an **episodic / temporal** character: retrospectively recalling a past thinking event or prospectively imagining a future processing state.

This excludes a huge class of systems that merely maintain an inaccessible scalar activation, contemporaneous diagnostic metric or time-agnostic self-state. A live counter without episodic access is a signal, not the sort of experience the authors selected.

**For the substrate:** a memory-mapped bit register becomes a self-model only if some declared reader relates those bits to an internal operation/state, retains enough identity/time to retrieve the episode, and can *use* that model to guide a later action. Do not anthropomorphize the ABI.

## 3. What exactly the review counted

The paper studies architecture *families* and named/unnamed examples, not one frozen implementation per name.

Examples of included CMA traditions:
- **Soar:** operator selection and episodic retrieval; potential to avoid repeated errors.
- **ACT-R:** episodic/instance-based learning, variable performance depending on task.
- **Meta-AQUA:** rich explanation-pattern traces and causal/temporal narrative links.
- **MIDCA:** object/meta-level cycles, anomaly detection, remedial planning.
- **CRAM:** ontological event representation, NEEM episodic memories and cross-modal grounding.
- **NARS:** internal experience encoded through Narsese, internal operations, feeling/attention/self-control.
- **BICA:** mental states and simulated perspectives.
- **Metacat:** problem-solving trace patterns and escape from stalled analogy attempts.
- **CARINA:** extensive traces of cognitive function input/output, priority, state and goals.
- **SALS:** mental execution as event graph.
- **MISM:** a cross-architecture metamodel, relatively thin on executable algorithms.
- **KS02:** an unusual adversarial case where metalevel anomaly detection must defend its own traces.

The paper's strongest contribution is **a map of diverse design decisions**, not the assertion that these systems are equivalent.

# Part II — Four arrangements of metacognition (Fig. 10, PDF p. 25)

## 4. Nelson–Narens: separate object-level and one meta-level

A meta-controller **monitors** first-order cognitive processing and can **control** it. Roughly **57.1%** of the surveyed CMAs follow this pattern (see Fig. 10a/11, §4.4).

Strengths: clean interface between observations and control decisions; easy to distinguish object execution from review policy. Risks: separate models drift; meta-controller misdescribes processes; telemetry may not capture true dependencies.

**lance-graph research analogy:** a dedicated diagnostic procedure over snapshot receipts could be the metalevel, but only if it has testable control effects. This is a design choice, not current code fact.

## 5. Unified: one mechanism regulates itself

No detached metalevel. The same inference machinery handles ordinary domain evidence and explicit representations of its own prior operations (Fig. 10b). The paper reports nearly a fifth of architectures using a unified model (§4.4).

Advantages: reuse of reasoning language and algorithms; a self-record can enter normal inference. Risks: circular evidence, self-reinforcement, recursive overhead, unverifiable post-hoc narration.

**NARS relevance:** the review classifies NARS with a unified model in its Fig. 11. More importantly, *Self in NARS* states that internal experience is Narsese, subject to ordinary reasoning.

**Substrate test:** can the same declared ISA/recipe machinery operate over a **trace-as-evidence** without conflating trace existence with truth of the underlying claim? Echo duplication is the essential negative control.

## 6. Tower: stacked meta-meta levels

Multiple levels regulate the next lower level (Fig. 10c). Attractive for monitoring one regulator by another; risks an infinite regress and exploding resources.

**Architectural warning:** adding an "audit of the auditor" cannot substitute for immutable receipts and a bounded policy contract. Stop at a finite, justified depth.

## 7. Federated: peer meta-levels

Multiple parallel meta-levels monitor and potentially regulate each other and the object-level (Fig. 10d). This can provide checks against a faulty controller, but brings races, authority ambiguity and divergent diagnoses.

**Research question:** can different critics independently validate a trace and produce a *single owned decision*? The one-subject authorization rule still applies: checking several owners is not authority to mutate each.

## 8. The unseen axis: automatic vs deliberate episode control

§4.4 notes another key split:
- Soar stores episodes automatically; later retrieval is operator-selected.
- Metacat selectively chooses which events deserve storage and may automatically clamp on trace matches.
- MIDCA/Meta-AQUA combine monitored traces and deliberate corrective behaviors.

**Do not infer the best policy from this review.** Instead, measure:
1. loss under sparse event selection;
2. missing key divergences after eviction;
3. compute/memory overhead per retained episode;
4. whether later self-diagnosis still succeeds.

# Part III — Trace representation: what is stored, and what is missing

## 9. Symbolic, hybrid and sub-symbolic

On the paper's CMA classifications (Fig. 13, §5.1):
- ≈ **56%** encode episodic metacognitive experience symbolically.
- ≈ **22%** use hybrid symbolic + sub-symbolic metadata.
- ≈ **3%** (one CMA) use purely sub-symbolic experience.
- Other cases are not fully classified; these percentages **do not sum to 100%**.

Hybrid examples attach confidence, activation, utility or indexing metrics to symbolic event traces; CRAM and ArmarX try to connect symbolic events to underlying sensor streams. The survey is not proof that symbolic is intrinsically superior; selection criteria favor inspectable self-models.

**lance-graph consequence:** a byte-aligned register can physically store scalars; a typed event graph can refer to those scalars. That is a representation choice. It does not certify that the system used the traces metacognitively.

## 10. Event, state, input, output, justification

Most traces are **event-centric** (§5.2):
- event type / invoked rule or operator;
- pre-state or inputs;
- post-state / output;
- task, priority, goal, and environment context;
- optional justification / fired rules;
- optional feelings, confidence, urgency or activation;
- time index or wall-clock timestamp;
- linkage to predecessor/successor and supporting evidence.

Examples directly from the review:
- CARINA records parameters, conditions, priorities, active state, goals, subgoals and visited states (§5.2).
- CRAM reifies input/output/premise/conclusion/performer as event-participant roles.
- SALS links knowledge-base updates to specific changed information.
- NARS preserves internal events; the cited NARS work uses elapsed intervals between successive events rather than a universal timestamp (§5.2).
- MIDCA interleaves mental actions and mental states.

**Critical separation for our system:**
`
EventId            = identity of a transition/receipt
EvidenceRootId     = identity of independent original support
DerivationLineage  = which roots and steps fed this transition
StateVersion       = immutable observation/snapshot
Owner/Principal    = who may mutate owned records
Recipe/Opcode      = executed operation, not guessed from result edge
SemanticReading    = interpretation of bytes, versioned separately from the ALU
`

These fields are a **proposed audit schema**, not a schema lifted from the survey and not a claim they are currently present in one object.

## 11. Linear sequence vs causal/temporal graph

The review contrasts ordered traces with explicit graph structures (§5.2):

- In a serial engine, chronology may approximate a sequence of operations.
- **Meta-AQUA** models links among states/processes (e.g. enabled, initiated, produced a result), allowing non-linear narratives.
- **SALS/Funk2** uses event graphs with parent/child relations.
- **CRAM** has temporal links and explicit, differentiated causal relation types.
- Graph structures help capture concurrency, multiple antecedents and alternatives.

**Warning:** a trace edge expressing *execution dependency* is not equivalent to Pearl's causal effect on the outside world. A process produced an output in a program execution; that alone does not prove the associated proposition *caused* an external event. Chronology is not causation.

## 12. Formality and ontologies

§5.4 reports **13 of 35** CMAs (≈37%) rely on schemas, formalisms or precise descriptions in the surveyed reports. Examples:
- CRAM: extension of SOMA OWL ontology, used in NEEM.
- CARINA: OWL for some profiling data.
- Meta-AQUA: XP-inspired graphlike patterns.
- NARS: its own formal knowledge language Narsese.
- MIDCA/ALMA: active logic for reasoning about steps.

Paper nuance: formal labels often organize trace components without a fully machine-checkable execution semantics.

**Architectural harvest:** record exact event ABI and reading version; provide a parser and executable verifier; do not equate "we have OWL labels" with formally validated reasoning.

## 13. Granularity, abstraction, sampling and eviction

Trace retention costs resources. Some CMAs store only salient events or compress several low-level operations into an abstract event; too much abstraction eliminates local failure evidence (§5.3, §7).

Proposed two-tier design (*inference, not paper's prescription*):
- **Hot resident:** small deterministic event/state summary sufficient for frequent ranking/diagnostics.
- **Cold durable:** append-only fine-grained receipts with content-addressed evidence roots and compaction-safe indexes.
- **Selective hydration:** expand the episode's dependency cone when the metamodel needs details.
- **Eviction rule:** may evict cached projections, not the last recoverable reference to a decisive observed root unless policy explicitly allows epistemic degradation.

**Falsifier:** compare complete vs progressively thinned traces on identical anomaly/revision scenarios. Track diagnosis recall, first-divergence accuracy, bytes, hydration latency, exact replay.

## 14. Trace legibility and faithful explanation

Authors emphasize that raw traces often remain inscrutable even to developers (§6.1). User-facing explanation is an extra transformation over the trace.

An explanation **must point back to committed operations**. Never generate a plausible looking chain that was not executed. The paper introduces this failure mode via an example of a language model's verbal explanation that did not match observed computation (§1).

Proposed evidence obligations:
1. the emitted explanation lists actual step/event IDs;
2. each step resolves to recorded inputs, instruction, state version and result;
3. replay of the claimed chain produces identical outputs;
4. absent receipts -> explicit refusal ("cannot explain from trace"), not invented internal narrative.

# Part IV — What systems DO with the traces

## 15. A five-phase metacognitive cycle

The paper's Fig. 14 (PDF p. 40) summarizes:
`
1. MODELLING  -> extract a self-model from monitored processing
2. DIAGNOSING -> detect discrepancy, anomaly, or repeated failure
3. PINPOINTING -> identify responsible component/step
4. STRATEGIZING -> select a bounded remedial action
5. REPAIRING   -> execute correction and observe subsequent behavior
`
This is a conceptual synthesis of surveyed CMAs; the precise algorithm differs per system.

**Most important missing joint in a mere logging substrate:**
`
logged episode -> faithful model -> falsifiable discrepancy
              -> source/step-localized explanation
              -> authorized change of recipe/priority/strategy
              -> measurement of changed outcome
`

The ability to *replay* is necessary but not sufficient.

## 16. Rule-based diagnostics

§6.3 describes predefined patterns or hand-authored rules:
- Meta-AQUA matches *Meta Explanation Patterns* (Meta-XPs) against process narratives.
- CARINA can compare current cognitive-function behavior with runtime expectations.
- KS02 constructs detectors for expected operations; missing essential events indicate corruption.
- Active-logic systems can expose predicates about a rule application at time t to the next inference step.

Strength: explicit and independently auditable. Weakness: hand-built pattern coverage and brittleness, especially under distribution drift.

**Proposed test:** insert a fault that makes one required operation silently disappear; evaluate whether the detector says "missing expected step" rather than projecting the expected output anyway.

## 17. Training-based anomaly detection

KS02 and other systems establish normal trace behavior first; deviations imply anomalies (§6.3.2).

Cautions:
- Learning a reference distribution does not guarantee detecting unknown failure modes.
- Self-observation can be corrupted by the same agent being observed.
- KS02 specifically explores protecting monitoring components from tampering, including meta-levels monitoring each other.
- A permanently learning detector risks normalizing the fault.

**Falsifiers:** corrupt the model but not the log; corrupt the log but not the model; manipulate one observer; gradually drift input frequency; measure false-positive rate and miss rate under fixed budgets.

## 18. Continuous adaptation / optimization

The survey includes architectures that change their processing strategies while operating:
- **SCADS:** introduces algorithmic variations (reported as about 5% of runs) and analyses traces for reusable simpler calculation strategies.
- **PRODIGY:** induces search-control rules when saved search time justifies future checking cost.
- **RCAA:** derives performance statistics from detailed and aggregated event histories.

No claim that any such system always improves. Exploration can initially harm performance; metacognitive overhead can exceed gains.

**Ablation rule:** measure time-to-correct-outcome, not only per-operation nanoseconds; include monitoring, logging, storage, hydration, decision and durable commit.

## 19. Case-based/explanation-based retrieval

Past episodes can provide precedent; Meta-AQUA and Metacat use patterns over prior traces; Soar episodic retrieval may help avoid repetitive dead ends.

Falsifier: feed two superficially similar failures with different causal origins. Retrieval must discriminate source/lineage and not execute an inapplicable repair because of surface similarity.

## 20. Hybrid fast detector, slow diagnosis

MIDCA combines a cheap learned anomaly signal with a more interpretive knowledge-based track (§6.3.3). The expensive branch is activated on anomalies; control phases include perception, interpretation, evaluation, intention, planning and action.

**Substrate harvest:** put cheap coincidence / residual / mask stages before an expensive counterfactual cone, but **measure stage-1 recall at a fixed false-positive rate**. If a fast gate silently drops a difficult case, downstream correctness does not save the whole system.

This connects directly to our Cascade Stroke-1 recall problem and the proposed Moore detector experiment. They are hypotheses for our own system, not statements made by Nolte et al.

# Part V — Empirical strength and the critical traps

## 21. Only six architectures had relevant quantified ablation evidence

§6.4 identifies quantitative with/without experiments for approaches used in:
1. ACT-R
2. AML11
3. BICA
4. CARINA
5. Meta-AQUA
6. MIDCA

= **6/35 ≈ 17%** for the question of metacognitive-experience use.

For five of these, a metacognitive-memory-equipped system generally outperformed its simpler comparator *in reported tasks*. ACT-R instance-based learning could hurt some tasks, helping on randomised/downscaled variations. An example in CARINA/FUNPRO reported 98% vs 71% valid resource retrieval in one evaluated setting; **not** a universal effect size.

The review cautions that removing metacognition may make some learning-based architectures unable to operate at all, producing a vacuous "with > without" result. A real control should retain as much baseline function as possible.

## 22. Baseline design that would improve on prior comparisons

Suggested *own* experimental matrix, same corpus, same resource budget:
| Arm | Internal event receipts | Self-model query | Diagnosis | Adaptive authorized repair |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | off | off | off | off |
| B | on | off | off | off |
| C | on | on | off | off |
| D | on | on | on | off |
| E | on | on | on | on |
| F | on, roots deliberately de-duplicated | on | on | on |
| G | on, compromised observer | on | on | on (should abstain) |

A/B tests *logging cost*; B/C tests *usable memory*; C/D tests *diagnosis*; D/E tests *behavioral utility*; E/F tests *provenance integrity*; G tests *monitoring trust*.

Endpoints:
- task success and calibrated abstention;
- diagnosis precision/recall, first divergence position, cone completeness;
- self-explanation receipt fidelity and hallucinated-step rate;
- confidence inflation for echo paths;
- time to recover after a deliberate fault;
- CPU cost, allocations, resident bytes, write amplification, batch commit latency;
- replay byte identity under same snapshot+reading+recipe;
- authorization failures and cross-owner mutation count.

Report **both** metacognitive quality and raw task score. A faster broken explanation is not an advance.

## 23. Epistemic versus operational outcomes

A trace can justify the claim *"this inference operation was executed"*. It does not thereby justify *"the resulting proposition is true"* or *"the chosen action caused the outcome"*.

Three orthogonal certificates should survive:
1. **execution**: signed/immutable receipt and reproducible state transition;
2. **epistemic**: valid, independent evidence supporting a proposition;
3. **causal/teleological**: separately warranted intervention, intention or purpose evidence.

This separation is an **inference for lance-graph**, motivated by paper examples of trace form and fallible introspection. Nolte et al. do not propose our CE64 encoding.

## 24. Methodological limitations that must remain visible

- Non-iterative initial query and heterogeneous vocabulary likely missed some projects.
- Data completeness varies strongly by architecture and item.
- 101 publications are not 101 independent experimental replications.
- Corpus under-represents opaque/end-to-end sub-symbolic agents under its explicit-experience selection criteria.
- Reported results overwhelmingly use narrow tasks, with few cross-CMA direct comparisons.
- Authors include researchers affiliated with CRAM/EASE, and journal paper declares the relevant potential interest.
- Newer research since the search window is not represented.
- Therefore *"metacognitive event logs measurably improve all systems"* is **not supported**.

# Part VI — Very concrete harvest for lance-graph

## 25. D1: Separate execution receipt, evidence root and self-model

**Minimal resident tuple, conceptual only:**
`
(snapshot, owner, row, lane, recipe_id, read_contract_version,
 event_id, evidence_roots_or_digest, before, after, diagnostic_kind)
`
All identifiers need explicit stability/compaction rules; do not pretend a six-bit witness or an ordinal is a globally stable root.

Store raw transitions with sparse append. Build the self-model as a **declared reading** over those transitions. A raw log record is historical data, not a mental proposition.

## 26. D2: Provenance and the echo falsifier

Scenario:
`
one observation O at root r
 -> inference A (EventId a)
 -> inference B (EventId b)
 -> both join in revision C
`
If A/B carry the same root r, independent-support aggregation must not count r twice. If a genuinely independent observation O2 arrives with root s, revision may increase evidence weight.

Measure both the exact F/C outputs and their quantization sensitivity. Fixing two paths to two distinct log coordinates without carrying roots is insufficient.

## 27. D3: Differentiated abstention

Metacognitive control can decide not to act for distinct reasons:
- not run / not scheduled;
- insufficient evidence;
- incomplete self-model;
- no replayable receipt;
- authorization denied;
- true violation of registered invariant;
- unresolved conflicting independent observers.

One external "abstain" interface is compatible with multiple internal causes. Neither a forbidden write nor a null log qualifies as epistemic uncertainty.

## 28. D4: Use first-divergence plus dependency cone

Compare original and cut replay under same snapshot.
`
cut -> original/counterfactual traces -> first_divergence
    -> per-step delta -> dependency cone -> diagnosed failure
    -> proposed (not yet authorized) repair
    -> replay under proposed change -> measured benefit
`
Score actual root-support reachability, not merely Boolean neighborhood coverage. A 64-plane bit-sliced resident population is suitable for **independent reachability scenarios**, not proof that the 64 truth replays ran.

## 29. D5: Need explicit control and authority

A moving focus is a computational actor. It is not an owner nor a principal merely because it produced a diagnosis. Decision to change a recipe must be separate from write authorization. #1491's single-subject principle remains intact.

Do not allow the controller to promote its own introspective claim to Causes, retroactively edit evidence, or mint permissions by deriving an intention.

## 30. D6: Three must-fail negative tests

**T-EXPLANATION-LAUNDER:** introduce a plausible unexecuted reasoning step into a generated explanation. Verified explanation must reject it.

**T-ECHO-AMPLIFY:** derive the same observation twice by disjoint routes. Confidence must not rise by double counting one root.

**T-PHANTOM-REPAIR:** modify metadata to claim improvement while leaving actual operations unchanged. Diagnosis must reject improvement without a rerun/observable outcome.

Each should have a deliberate disable-run making its guard red.

## 31. D7: Cognitive economy is part of the outcome

The paper's motivation includes limited resource cognition; measure self-monitoring overhead:
- hot vs cold state fraction;
- number of events sampled and missed;
- number of hydrated cones;
- appends and compaction;
- variation by reasoning depth and number of owners.

No claim like "7 ns per committed event" can stand if durability occurs per 1.7 ms batch. Compare equivalent stages.

# Part VII — Traceable experiment and acceptance bar

## 32. Minimal eight-case self-model fixture pack

1. **Correct inference, trace present:** faithful explanation resolves full inputs.
2. **Correct inference, trace absent:** answer may remain correct; explanation abstains.
3. **Wrong inference, detectable missing step:** detector localizes it.
4. **Wrong inference, undetectable by cheap filter:** report recall loss before deep path.
5. **Two derivations from one root:** evidence counted once.
6. **Two independent observations:** evidence increases correctly.
7. **Self-optimization causes regression:** revert/abstain; do not report universal improvement.
8. **Corrupted logger / untrusted meta-observer:** no self-certification without independent receipt.

For each: attach exact snapshot, version, provenance graph, legal operation, expected reaction, result and ablation effect. Use deterministic seeds, keep observer state isolated and pin outputs byte-for-byte where feasible.

## 33. Scientific claim ladder

- **S0 represented:** a log/schema exists.
- **S1 replayed:** same inputs reproduce the trace.
- **S2 interpreted:** a declared self-model answers queries about that trace.
- **S3 diagnostic:** a discrepancy/defect is localized and independently validated.
- **S4 corrective:** a legal change alters subsequent execution.
- **S5 useful:** controlled ablation proves improved measurable outcomes without hidden extra compute.
- **S6 generalizable:** survives held-out tasks, drift and architecture variants.

This is an *evaluation rubric proposed here*, not terminology asserted by Nolte et al. A system with receipts alone must not claim S4–S6.

## 34. Preferred immediate action

Write **one experiment**, not a new metacognition library:
- capture one complete NARS/CE64/Thought recipe episode;
- deliberately remove or corrupt a supporting root;
- compute first divergence and a provenance-aware cone;
- have a declared self-reader diagnose the cause;
- propose an authorized recipe-level change;
- rerun and show which claim changes, with a receipt;
- ablate the self-reader and demonstrate the difference **on the same task budget**.

If the baseline already succeeds without diagnosis, choose a harder scenario where the added meta-loop has an opportunity to help, but record this selection to avoid cherry-picking.
