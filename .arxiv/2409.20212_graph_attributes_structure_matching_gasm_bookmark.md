## Source abstract

The work proposes **GASM (Graph Attributes and Structure Matching)**, incorporating connectivity and vertex/edge attributes when searching for vertex correspondences between different graphs. It introduces parameters to account for attribute reliability and compares matching quality/timing to baseline graph-matching methods. The abstract reports performance competitive with state-of-the-art methods, including on structure-only problems.

No proof of a perfect or generally lossless match is claimed here. Empirical results have not been independently replicated in this note.

## Relevance to the canonical thought address decision

**Do not apply graph matching where none is needed.** All 64 Lance-graph thoughts share the same 65,536 canonical row addresses in a sealed version: Thought[a][i] and Thought[b][i] already agree on the canonical coordinate. Presence mask intersection and positional folds do not need GASM or a new matching index.

Potential *external* uses: aligning independently minted ontologies, imported entity graphs, schema families or external datasets whose identities cannot be assumed equal. Attribute reliability may help generate candidates, but exact canonical identity still requires an explicit verified binding and preservation of alternatives.

**Probe for later:** Two distinct graphs with intentionally ambiguous structure and conflicting attributes; include false-friend identity, partial isomorphism, missing edges, duplicated labels, and low-reliability attributes. Compare to an independent oracle. Never silently merge NodeGuid identities based solely on matching score.

**Boundary:** Research bookmark only, not evidence that GASM is used or needed in the common-coordinate hot path.
