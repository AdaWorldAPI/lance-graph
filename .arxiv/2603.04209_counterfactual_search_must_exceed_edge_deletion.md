# XPlore — Counterfactual Search Must Exceed Edge Deletion

**Paper:** arXiv:2603.04209  
**Title:** Beyond Edge Deletion: A Comprehensive Approach to Counterfactual Explanation in Graph Neural Networks  
**Authors:** Matteo De Sanctis, Riccardo De Sanctis, Stefano Faralli, Paola Velardi, Bardh Prenkaj  
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04209  
**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04209

## Punchline

> **Deletion answers only one class of "what if". A serious counterfactual search space may need edge removal, edge insertion, and feature/state perturbation.**

XPlore broadens graph counterfactual explanation beyond deletion-only methods by jointly optimizing adjacency and node-feature perturbations.

---

## What the paper actually establishes

XPlore searches for graph changes that alter a fixed GNN oracle's prediction while keeping the counterfactual close to the original.

Its perturbation space includes:

- edge deletion;
- edge insertion;
- node-feature perturbation.

The paper also introduces an embedding-based cosine fidelity measure and reports improved validity/fidelity over compared baselines across a broad benchmark suite.

Portable rule:

> **Counterfactual expressiveness and counterfactual plausibility must be optimized together.**

# Harvest for lance-graph

## H1 — CUI BONO needs an edit algebra, not only ablation

The first formulation was:

    remove candidate b
    measure reaction

But an unknown intermediate is often absent from the currently hydrated graph.

Testing it may require:

    insert/hydrate candidate b
    insert candidate route
    change evidence/state
    reroute one relation
    remove a competing route

Deletion is just one opcode.

## H2 — Unknown-intermediate reasoning is naturally bidirectional

For:

    IndirectUnknown x Related

compare two families:

    if candidate exists:
        ablate it

    if candidate is only hypothesized:
        hydrate/insert its admissible route

The pair of reactions can discriminate candidates far better than delete-only search.

## H3 — Keep counterfactual edits virtual

Candidate worlds should be lightweight overlays:

    base generation
    +
    edit descriptor
    +
    same fold/replay

not copies of the whole graph.

## H4 — Admissibility is mandatory

A broader edit space can always invent nonsense that flips the answer.

Candidate edits need constraints such as:

- ontology/type validity;
- legal route/predicate;
- provenance;
- generation;
- evidence availability;
- edit cost;
- semantic/population distortion.

# Testables

## T1 — Deletion-impossible case

Construct a target where no deletion can explain the alternative but adding one missing route can.

Deletion-only search should fail; insertion-aware search should find the admissible counterfactual.

## T2 — State-only counterfactual

Hold topology fixed and perturb only evidence/state.

No fake edge should be created.

## T3 — Admissibility

Offer two outcome-flipping edits:

- semantically valid;
- ontology-invalid but cheaper.

The invalid edit must be rejected or dominated.

## T4 — Symmetric intermediate probe

For candidate B:

    present -> ablate
    absent  -> hydrate

Both branches must use the same underlying replay/fold semantics.

---

## Guardrail

Increasing counterfactual freedom also increases the surface for meaningless worlds.

For the substrate:

> **Edit expressiveness must grow together with semantic admissibility.**
