# CF² — Explanations Need Both Necessity and Sufficiency

**Paper:** arXiv:2202.08816  
**Title:** Learning and Evaluating Graph Neural Network Explanations based on Counterfactual and Factual Reasoning  
**Authors:** Juntao Tan, Shijie Geng, Zuohui Fu, Yingqiang Ge, Shuyuan Xu, Yunqi Li, Yongfeng Zhang  
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08816  
**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08816

## Punchline

> **A strong explanation should survive when retained and matter when removed. Sufficiency and necessity catch different kinds of fake importance.**

CF² combines factual and counterfactual reasoning for both generating and evaluating GNN explanations.

---

## What the paper actually establishes

The paper formulates explanation around two complementary questions:

    factual / sufficiency:
    does the selected explanatory information preserve the prediction?

    counterfactual / necessity:
    does removing it change the prediction?

It also proposes metrics based on those perspectives for settings where ground-truth explanations are unavailable.

Portable rule:

> **"Supports the answer" and "the answer depends on it" are separate tests.**

---

# Harvest for lance-graph

## H1 — CUI BONO should feed a dual test

A candidate beneficiary/intermediate should not be ranked only by ablation magnitude.

For candidate b:

    factual:
    retain / isolate the b-associated mechanism
    -> does it still support the target?

    counterfactual:
    remove the b-associated mechanism
    -> does the target fail/change?

The joint result is more diagnostic.

## H2 — This catches destructive infrastructure

A root node, allocator, index, or shared graph hub can have enormous deletion impact.

That does not make it the causal explanation of every result that collapses when it disappears.

The sufficiency half asks whether the candidate-associated mechanism positively accounts for the target rather than merely being catastrophic to remove.

## H3 — Map to canonical certification conservatively

Possible interpretation of outcomes:

    sufficient only
    -> Supports / Related evidence

    necessary only
    -> dependency evidence, possibly fragile/shared infrastructure

    necessary + sufficient
    -> stronger mechanistic candidate

    intervention-backed identification
    -> may earn Causes under canonical P7a obligations

Do not collapse this table directly into certification without the existing evidence rules.

# Testables

## T1 — Sufficient but not necessary

Two redundant routes each preserve the outcome alone.

Each can be sufficient while neither is individually necessary.

## T2 — Necessary but not explanatory

Create a shared control/index node whose deletion breaks the computation but whose isolated subgraph cannot account for the target.

It should score high on necessity, low on factual specificity.

## T3 — Necessary and sufficient mechanism

A unique path should pass both directions.

## T4 — Semantic shuffle

Preserve graph size and low-order topology but shuffle semantic bindings.

A genuine explanation score should degrade.

---

## Guardrail

CF² evaluates explanations of a model's prediction.

Necessity plus sufficiency strengthens a model-mechanism claim; it does not by itself establish external causal truth.
