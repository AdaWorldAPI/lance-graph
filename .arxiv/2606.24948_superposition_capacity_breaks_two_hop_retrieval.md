## Punchline

> **An algebraically reversible binding operation is not a guarantee that an individual fact survives superposition well enough to support the next reasoning step.**

One can successfully recover the intermediate node of a two-hop path and still fail because the next atomic fact has already become difficult to retrieve. The authors identify retrieval capacity/interference as the principal bottleneck in the tested global-memory architecture.

Do not confuse this with another “hologram” paper, [arXiv:2408.10592](2408.10592_hologram_is_a_graph_theorem_matches_must_preserve_witnesses.md): that paper's hologram is a heterogeneous geometry graph with explicit theorem patterns, not a VSA.

## What the source establishes (paper abstract)

### S1. Architecture: two holographic memory variants with cleanup

The study compares:

- **HRR**, a real-valued Holographic Reduced Representation using circular convolution for symbol binding/unbinding.
- **FHRR**, a phase-only Fourier HRR operating on complex phases.
- Both use a modern Hopfield-style cleanup stage to turn a noisy recovered vector into a plausible entity.

The system is trained/evaluated on **FB15k-237** for **five seeds**. It targets unseen two-hop relation chains, not ordinary exact Datalog path enumeration.

**Important dimensions note:** prior conversation discussed a 1,024-real-coordinate HRR setting and 512 complex phases for FHRR. The abstract does not specify these widths; mark them *pending verification against the full paper or code* rather than treating this file as an independent source for those numbers.

### S2. Single-hop retrieval is meaningful but insufficient

Reported filtered MRR:

| System | Single-hop filtered MRR |
|---|---:|
| HRR | **0.358 ± 0.002** |
| FHRR | **0.350 ± 0.021** |

MRR is mean reciprocal rank, not exact top-1 accuracy and not logical certainty. The two models have some useful single-hop ranking behavior in the study's protocol.

### S3. Zero-shot multi-hop composition fails

The paper reports two-hop accuracy at roughly chance across tested cleanup temperatures. These are unseen chains, without direct chain-specific training.

The failure is not explained away simply by poor recovery of the first intermediate node.

### S4. Intermediate recovery is strong

For HRR, the first-hop diagnostic returns the relevant intermediate with **MRR 0.896 ± 0.002**.

Even supplying a verified-correct intermediate does not make the downstream composition succeed reliably. That implicates the available second-hop information rather than only propagation of the first-hop error.

### S5. A standalone second-hop probe already fails

The decisive experiment extracts the ground-truth hop-2 fact and asks for it as a **single atomic query**, bypassing path composition.

Its recovery is only **0.26–0.48× of the average atomic accuracy**. The paper reports this across relation fan-out strata.

This is the useful localization:

~~~text
hop1 mostly recovered
       ↓
correct intermediate supplied
       ↓
hop2 still fails
       ↓
standalone hop2 fact itself hard to retrieve
       ↓
capacity / interference under shared superposition
~~~

The conclusion is about the **tested learned global-memory pipeline**; it does not establish that HRR/FHRR generally cannot compose or that all vector symbolic architectures fail.

### S6. Cleanup does not preserve arbitrary phase binding (Lemma 4.1)

The paper proves that FHRR softmax cleanup is generally **not phase-equivariant**:

~~~text
Clean(z ⊙ r) != Clean(z) ⊙ r
~~~

Here \`⊙\` is FHRR phase binding. Thus a local cleanup can disturb the algebra assumed at the following step.

This compounds failure on some paths; according to the paper it is **secondary** to impaired atomic retrieval.

## What is NOT established by this source

- That all 1,024-dimensional vectors are “low quality,” or that 10,000 / 16,384 dimensions would solve the problem.
- That performance increases linearly with dimension; no dimensional sweep is established by the inspected abstract.
- That retrieval from a single superposed vector is comparable in storage budget or query semantics to addressable 7^7 × 16Kbit crystals.
- That exact Datalog joins solve *withheld-fact prediction*: exact joins can only compose facts that are actually present unless additional inferential rules are supplied.
- That signed pentary cancellation, binary Hamming, or local address sharding beats FHRR without a controlled comparison.
- That FHRR binding is *itself* lossy for one exact operation. Binding and **superposed retrieval** are different sources of error.

## Engineering harvest: preserve source identity, compress the selection

Current lance-graph hypothesis:

~~~text
known addressable observations, one explicit evidence version
        ↓
 typed consumer / relation query
        ↓
 symbolic selection + presence masks
        ↓
 authorized row-address prefilter / positional reads
        ↓
 typed fold / Datalog join / NAL evidence revision
        ↓
 answer + source witness + replay certificate
~~~

Compact VSA/phase/centroid codes could remain as optional candidate-prioritization consumers, rather than the **sole copy** of all factual information.

H14d's existing results support mask-before-read mechanics, NOT arbitrary free two-hop joins and NOT a uniform page-I/O win. Uniform sparse survivors can still touch nearly every physical Lance page; clustering matters at page granularity.

The key invariant to test is:

~~~text
IdentityAddressed(fact) != ApproximateCleanup(fact)
SourceWitness(fact)     != NewlyCreatedEvidence(fact)
ExactJoin              != PredictMissingFact
~~~
