# Large growth happens: numerical Gaussian elimination is not Boolean fold algebra

**Source:** Daniel A. Spielman and Xifan Yu, *Large Growth Happens: Gaussian Elimination with Partial Pivoting on Random Matrices*, [arXiv:2610.08701v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08701), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08701v1); 2026-10-06. Abstract, §§1, 2.1–2.2.  
**Research status (2026-10-21):** Primary source read; numerical-stability negative-control source, not a claimed QR speedup.

## Source-grounded excerpt

The paper establishes a nontrivial **lower bound** on the probability of large element growth during Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting (GEPP), for random Gaussian matrices. It rules out certain previously conjectured fast-decaying tails. It works with *real-valued* numerical matrices and floating-point stability analysis, not matrices over GF(2) and not existential Boolean relations.

A growth factor compares the largest value in the upper triangular factor with the largest value in the input. Even if factorization gives a formally correct decomposition, floating-point results can lose accuracy when intermediate magnitudes grow.

## Hypothetical relevance

Explicitly type every proposed elimination `AlgebraKind`: REAL_F64, GF2_XOR, BOOL_OR_AND, COUNT_BAG, or other. Never let an e-graph or 8-bit method palette silently exchange associativity/rewrites between floating-point and exact finite/Boolean domains.

In the user's illustrative `solve_lgs` implementation, partial pivoting is sensible, but `pivot.abs() < 1e-12` is an absolute cutoff and scale-dependent. Validate all input rows, reject nonfinite entries and test residual/error on badly scaled and near-singular cases.

## Falsifiers / test path N

- **N1, real systems:** Wilkinson-like and other adverse-growth matrices, near-singular, badly scaled, exact and ill-conditioned controls. Check residual norm, relative solution error, growth factor and pivot cutoff sensitivity against a high-precision reference.
- **N2, metamorphic scaling:** Multiply both A and b by a small/large nonzero scalar. Mathematical solution is unchanged; a fixed absolute pivot cutoff can yield different refusal status. This is an expected negative finding to fix, not a numerical success.
- **N3, rewrite legality:** For f64, reassociation may change results; the cold extractor either attaches a proven tolerance bound or refuses exact-proof labeling. Contrast with GF(2) XOR and Boolean OR/AND, which are exact in their respective domains.
- **Silence twin:** An unchanged, version-pinned QR256 Boolean mask must not incur floating-point pivot work.

**Verdict:** Strong **guardrail** for any f64 path; paper makes no claim about graph-fold throughput.
