# Gaussian elimination versus greedy synthesis: exact reversible linear transforms

**Source:** Timothée Goubault de Brugière et al., *Gaussian Elimination versus Greedy Methods for the Synthesis of Linear Reversible Circuits*, [arXiv:2201.06508v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06508), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.06508v1), abstract, §1, conclusion. Submitted 2022-01-17, journal reference 2021.  
**Research status (2026-10-21):** Primary-source excerpt, research only. Paraphrases, not copied passages. **No Lance-graph benchmark or runtime admission.**

## Source-grounded excerpt

The paper synthesizes *linear reversible circuits*, representable by invertible matrices over GF(2). It compares Gaussian elimination coupled with tuned LU/greedy selection against purely greedy circuit synthesis. The claimed output improvements are CNOT **count** and **circuit depth**, not CPU speed for relational joins. Reported preference: an optimized Gaussian/greedy method on random operators above 150 wires; purely greedy methods are near-optimal in their small-domain trials below 30 wires. Both ranges are from this paper's circuits, not transferable performance thresholds.

Elementary row additions over GF(2) are XORs. Reversibility matters because a sequence of elementary operations can be recorded or inverted. The representation is not the Boolean OR/AND semiring used for existential Datalog joins.

## Hypothetical transfer to lance-graph

Use **operation selection and schedule extraction** as an idea: given a GF(2)-typed 256-column matrix, produce a compact palette-coded sequence of row-XOR, swap, and pivot choices. Compare to naive row-by-row elimination, an M4RI-style blocked baseline and a scalar reference. Treat the schedule as a proof witness, not a record of subjective reasoning.

For relalg existential joins, use separate AND/OR rules; an XOR elimination schedule is **not** interchangeable with relational existence.

## Falsifiable tests

- **G1, GF(2) parity:** For random and singular 8/64/256-column inputs, apply the extracted elementary-operation schedule and independently verify `E · A = R` under GF(2). If E is not preserved, replay the logged elementary steps; do not claim inversion for singular input.
- **G2, no false semiring parity:** Select a two-path graph with two derivations of the same target. XOR yields 0, OR yields 1. Any attempt to reuse the XOR schedule as a Datalog EXISTS kernel must refuse or fail this negative oracle.
- **G3, cost model:** Compare CNOT-oriented schedule length with actual scalar/SIMD wall time, cold plan cost, replay bytes and cache traffic. A shorter circuit is *not* automatically faster on the CPU.
- **Silence twin:** A no-op row addition or a permuted list of independent operations must not emit a committed QR dirty bit.
