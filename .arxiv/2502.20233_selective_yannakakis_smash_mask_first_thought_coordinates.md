# Selective Yannakakis / SMASH: choose when to reduce before joining

**Paper:** Daniela Böhm, Georg Gottlob, Matthias Lanzinger, Davide Longo, Cem Okulmus, Reinhard Pichler, Alexander Selzer, *Selective Use of Yannakakis' Algorithm to Improve Query Performance: Machine Learning to the Rescue*.  
**Source:** [arXiv:2502.20233v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20233v2) | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.20233v2) | [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20233v2) (revised 2025-06-20).  
**Read status:** Source-grounded selective review of §§I–II, IV–VI, not independently replicated. **Date:** 2026-10-19.  
**Implementation status:** Research only. No code, compiler, CE64 or existing documentation modified.

## Paper: claims and scope

The authors study the **algorithm-selection** problem: apply Yannakakis-style semijoin reduction only where it helps query runtime. For a join-tree **acyclic** conjunctive query, the classical algorithm performs a bottom-up semijoin pass, a top-down semijoin pass and then joins reduced relations.

For eligible **0MA** (zero-materialization-answerable) aggregate queries, *guardedness* and *set-safety* allow the answer to come from the guard after only the bottom-up semijoins. Examples of set-safe aggregates include MIN, MAX and COUNT DISTINCT; ordinary COUNT(*) and bag-sensitive aggregates are **not** automatically equivalent.

Motivating §I SQL example: PostgreSQL original **3.38 s** vs rewritten **0.11 s**; changing one predicate switches the preferred plan, original **0.05 s** vs rewritten **0.09 s**. A faster theoretical operator may lose on physical overhead.

The authors' MEAMBench contains **4,677** queries after augmentation. Their supervised selector **SMASH** is evaluated with PostgreSQL, DuckDB and Spark SQL. §VI-D reports minor slowdowns on **over half** of test queries when always rewriting, versus about **2%** with SMASH on its evaluated test workload. The authors emphasize avoiding **false positives** in applying the optimization.

The paper does **not** benchmark Lance-graph, Lance 13, R2IL, mask-risc, or a shared Cartesian thought address space. Its times and speedup factors must not be presented as our measurements.

## Lance-graph: directly relevant existing evidence (independent of the paper)

All 64 active thoughts share **65,536 canonical row coordinates** in one sealed version. For selected aligned thought populations, eliminating nonparticipating rows is direct mask algebra, not foreign-key lookup or trie search.

~~~text
R = Aperture AND Presence[A] AND Presence[B] AND NOT Exclusion[C]
for each address i in R:
    fold(Thought[A][i], Thought[B][i])
~~~

COUNT/EXISTS may terminate on the mask (popcount/any) without a payload read. A complete terminal needs values only at surviving addresses. The identical canonical address is the join witness; equal arbitrary numeric offsets across unrelated domains are not.

- [#1383](https://github.com/AdaWorldAPI/lance-graph/pull/1383): existing gated semijoin through MaskOp::Gather under a gate. **Not** a recommendation to resurrect forbidden foreign-value gather designs.
- [#1573](https://github.com/AdaWorldAPI/lance-graph/pull/1573): thought-first positional reads, Hamming presence and Lance 13 masked I/O (probe).
- [#1575](https://github.com/AdaWorldAPI/lance-graph/pull/1575): RelAlg normalize -> address R -> Lance 13 RowAddrMask -> terminal (measured probe).
- [#1587](https://github.com/AdaWorldAPI/lance-graph/pull/1587): GYO α-acyclicity test plus a cyclic case where semijoin reduction keeps all rows although the full join is empty.
- [#1590](https://github.com/AdaWorldAPI/lance-graph/pull/1590): shared-coordinate pair is positional; foreign-index/value-addressed gather proposals are marked VIOLATION.
- [#1599](https://github.com/AdaWorldAPI/lance-graph/pull/1599), [#1605](https://github.com/AdaWorldAPI/lance-graph/pull/1605): partial thought squash / Git-like lifecycle remain design proposals, **not** existing physical join execution.

Lance 13 Scanner::with_row_addr_prefilter accepts irregular RowAddrMask allow-lists, not just contiguous ranges. However, #1575 finds byte-read ratios of **1.00× at uniform 1%**, **0.19× at clustered 1%**, **0.99× at clustered 10%**. Arbitrary logical masks do not guarantee proportional page I/O reductions. Account separately for conversion from R to Lance row IDs, which was outside the timed scanner call.

## Research-transfer hypothesis, not a paper conclusion

A query plan selector could choose among (1) common-coordinate positional mask fold, (2) semijoin reduction over **proven** separator projections for real multi-variable joins, and (3) direct execution. A 0MA shortcut requires demonstrated guardedness and set-safety, including exact bag-multiplicity handling when applicable.

**Falsification suite:** Compare all three at identical answers under (a) aligned rows, (b) unmatched unrelated row universes with the same integer offsets (must refuse), (c) valid acyclic join tree, (d) cyclic #1587 counterexample, (e) duplicate/bag cases, and (f) clustered vs scattered Lance addresses with stale-version mapping. Measure end-to-end wall time, source bytes, conversion, page locality, read amplification and optimizer regret. Compare simple deterministic selection before adding a learned selector or MUL calibration.

**Boundary:** A masked positional intersection is already available for canonical aligned thought populations; general Yannakakis variable-separator propagation is not proven equivalent to plain AND between unrelated relation domains. No new interpreter or production code authorized.
