# Towards World Models in Biomedical Research — World Model as Replayable Intervention Algebra

**Paper:** arXiv:2606.05925  
**Title:** Towards World Models in Biomedical Research  
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05925  
**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05925

## Punchline

> **A world model is not the stored world. It is the replayable algebra that separates what is observed, what is predicted, what would happen under intervention, and what reality eventually permits us to believe.**

The paper proposes biomedical world models as systems that represent biological state, simulate intervention-conditioned future trajectories, and use those simulations to plan informative experiments or actions.

For lance-graph, the important harvest is not “build another learned latent simulator.” It is a smaller executable contract:

~~~text
BoundState
   ×
Intervention
   ×
Recipe
   ×
Context
        ↓
VirtualTransition
~~~

with hard boundaries:

~~~text
VirtualTransition
    cannot certify itself
    cannot mutate its parent
    is replayable
    may remain unmaterialized
    carries epistemic provenance
~~~

Only an external observation followed by revision may change the certified world state.

The smallest substrate reading is therefore:

> **simulate → intervene → observe → revise**

never:

> **simulate → believe**

## What the paper actually establishes

### 1. Biomedical world models are transition models, not static predictors

The paper frames biomedical world models around latent biological state plus intervention-conditioned dynamics.

The essential distinction is:

~~~text
observations O<=t
      ↓
infer / bind state z_t
      ↓
candidate intervention a_t
      ↓
transition model
      ↓
virtual future z_t+1
      ↓
predicted observable outcome
~~~

The model is useful because it can simulate alternative biological futures before an intervention is actually performed.

Portable rule:

> **The intervention must be an explicit argument of the transition, not an annotation attached after prediction.**

For lance-graph this suggests that a reasoning/action ordinal can participate directly in the state-transition coordinate.

---

### 2. Prediction and observation are different epistemic objects

A simulated future is not itself an observation.

This separation is easy to blur in a self-revising reasoning system:

~~~text
predict X
  ↓
store X as knowledge
  ↓
X now supports future predictions
  ↓
confidence rises
~~~

That is an epistemic feedback loop.

The portable invariant is:

> **A prediction may create virtual state, but it may not create observational evidence about itself.**

For the substrate:

~~~text
fold / causal propagation / 0..63
        ↓
     prediction
        ↓
   no self-certification
        ↓
measurement / experiment / external observation
        ↓
       SPOFC
        ↓
      revision
~~~

This is one of the strongest architecture constraints to harvest from the paper.

---

### 3. Intervention creates branches from a shared bound state

A world model must support different futures from the same current state under different interventions.

Conceptually:

~~~text
                 z_t
              /   |   \
          do(A) do(B) do(C)
            ↓     ↓     ↓
           zA    zB    zC
~~~

For lance-graph the branch identity should remain explicit:

~~~text
same generation
+ same semantic coordinate
+ different intervention
────────────────────────────
different virtual branch
~~~

The parent state must remain immutable while branches are explored.

This is a natural fit for replayable reasoning generations and deterministic recipe ordinals.

---

### 4. Latent rollout and fold-first architecture share the same pressure

The paper motivates latent-space dynamics partly because long rollouts should not require reconstructing a complete high-dimensional observation at every step.

The transferable systems principle is:

> **Do not materialize a complete observable world merely to perform another internal transition.**

For lance-graph:

~~~text
z0
 ↓ transition/fold
z1
 ↓ transition/fold
z2
 ↓ transition/fold
z3
 ↓
materialize only at an observation/debug/commit boundary
~~~

This aligns directly with the existing direction:

~~~text
fold first
mask second
materialize last
~~~

The paper uses learned latent state as the compression mechanism. lance-graph may test whether an executable algebra over compact state can satisfy the same systems requirement without adopting a monolithic learned latent representation.

That is a lance-graph hypothesis, not a claim made by the paper.

---

### 5. Hybrid state should preserve different information kinds

The paper argues that biomedical world models must combine heterogeneous information: molecular, cellular, tissue, clinical, imaging, physiological, and structured knowledge.

Its discussion allows both continuous and discrete representations rather than forcing every state variable into one representation.

For the substrate this encourages a separation such as:

~~~text
semantic identity / ontology
        ↓
ordinal / class / relation

measurement / similarity / evidence
        ↓
quantized evidence / compact numeric field

population
        ↓
mask + fold

dynamics
        ↓
transition / causal recipe
~~~

Portable rule:

> **Do not flatten semantic identity, quantitative evidence, and transition semantics into one undifferentiated embedding unless the loss of structure is justified.**

---

### 6. Plausible rollout is not causal fidelity

The paper explicitly separates realistic-looking prediction from causal validity.

A model can predict the next likely observation while still encoding the wrong intervention mechanism.

This matters directly for the reasoning band:

~~~text
correlation
    !=
predictive transition
    !=
interventional support
    !=
counterfactual causal license
~~~

Portable invariant:

> **No amount of observational agreement may silently promote a claim into a causal class.**

This is the world-model analogue of:

> agreement != truth

from the SCOUT harvest.

For bits 61..63, the paper therefore supports treating higher reasoning states as different epistemic licenses rather than simply larger confidence values.

The exact bit assignment remains a substrate design decision.

---

### 7. Counterfactual validity requires branch discipline

A counterfactual is meaningful only if alternative futures are derived from the same relevant starting state while varying the intended intervention.

For deterministic reasoning:

~~~text
parent generation G
      ↓
bind state
   ┌──┴────────┐
 do(A)        do(B)
   ↓            ↓
 branch A     branch B
~~~

Neither branch may contaminate the other or mutate G merely because it was simulated.

Portable rule:

> **Counterfactual exploration must be side-effect free until an explicit observation/revision boundary is crossed.**

This is a useful hard contract for replayable thought.

---

### 8. Planning should value information gain, not only predicted reward

The paper discusses scientific planning in terms that include expected information gain, uncertainty reduction, utility, feasibility, cost, and safety.

That is important for Rubikon-like scheduling.

A useful experiment can be one that is not predicted to produce the “best” outcome, but instead cleanly separates competing explanations.

For lance-graph:

~~~text
candidate recipe/intervention
        ↓
virtual rollout
        ↓
expected entropy reduction?
expected disagreement collapse?
causal discrimination?
cost?
risk?
        ↓
scheduler
~~~

Portable rule:

> **Where uncertainty can be reduced is a scheduling signal, not merely a confidence score.**

This gives Shannon-style uncertainty a clean role: point toward where another observation is valuable.

---

### 9. Closed-loop world models require reality ingress

The paper's scientific loop is not complete at simulation.

The useful loop is:

~~~text
hypothesis
   ↓
simulate possible futures
   ↓
choose intervention / experiment
   ↓
perform action in the real system
   ↓
observe success, failure, or surprise
   ↓
update the model
~~~

Failure cases are therefore first-class information.

For the substrate:

~~~text
prediction
    ↓
opportunity to be wrong
    ↓
discrepancy
    ↓
revision
~~~

A failed prediction is not garbage. It is a high-value epistemic event.

---

### 10. Evaluation must cross several independent axes

The paper calls for evaluation beyond next-step predictive accuracy, including state consistency, temporal coherence, interventional accuracy, counterfactual validity, and downstream planning usefulness.

A lance-graph translation is:

| World-model property | Substrate question |
|---|---|
| Observational realism | Does predicted measurement match later SPOFC / experiment? |
| State consistency | Does semantic identity remain bound across views and modalities? |
| Temporal coherence | Is the same generation/recipe replay stable? |
| Interventional accuracy | Does do(X) produce the expected observed change? |
| Counterfactual validity | Do alternative branches differ for the right causal reason? |
| Planning utility | Does the scheduler choose experiments that actually resolve uncertainty? |

These should remain distinct metrics. One strong dimension must not silently certify another.

# Harvest for lance-graph

## H1 — No self-certifying prediction

Hard invariant:

~~~text
prediction × any number of replays != observation
~~~

Repeated internal simulation may improve consistency estimates, but may not fabricate new empirical evidence.

---

## H2 — Make intervention part of replay identity

A deterministic replay key should conceptually include:

~~~text
ReplayKey =
    generation
  × semantic coordinate
  × intervention
  × recipe ordinal
  × bound context
  × allowed randomness contract
~~~

Then:

~~~text
same ReplayKey -> same VirtualTransition
~~~

is falsifiable.

---

## H3 — Virtual branches remain virtual

A counterfactual branch should be representable without creating a certified state generation.

Possible conceptual result:

~~~text
VirtualTransition {
    parent_generation,
    intervention,
    recipe,
    predicted_delta,
    epistemic_license,
    provenance,
}
~~~

This does not require a new storage type. It can remain a logical contract over existing state/fold machinery.

---

## H4 — Keep observation ingress explicit

The world model may propose:

~~~text
predicted_delta
~~~

but only an external observation path may produce evidence used for certification:

~~~text
external observation
        ↓
SPOFC / evidence binding
        ↓
revision
        ↓
certified generation
~~~

This preserves the distinction between imagination and measurement.

---

## H5 — Keep causal grading orthogonal to agreement

A useful ordering is:

~~~text
agreement/disagreement
        ↓
replayable evidence
        ↓
provenance / independence
        ↓
intervention tests
        ↓
counterfactual consistency
        ↓
causal license
~~~

Do not let large observational populations or repeated agreement promote a claim into a causal tier by arithmetic alone.

---

## H6 — Let Rubikon schedule falsification opportunities

The scheduler should be able to prefer actions that maximize discrimination between live hypotheses.

Conceptually:

~~~text
unresolved state
      ↓
candidate interventions
      ↓
simulate branch outcomes
      ↓
which observation would split hypotheses most?
      ↓
schedule that experiment
~~~

This is stronger than reward maximization because it makes being proven wrong useful.

---

## H7 — Preserve compact rollout until a boundary matters

World-model state should stay in the smallest executable representation that can support the next transition.

Materialize only for:

- external observation comparison;
- debugging/inspection;
- explicit certification;
- a downstream consumer that proves it needs the population.

This is the systems-level bridge from the paper's latent simulation motivation to fold-first execution.

# Testables

## T1 — Prediction isolation

Create a predicted edge/state that has no external observation.

### Expected

It can participate in virtual rollout but cannot appear as observational evidence in the certified generation.

### Falsifies

Any path by which a model-generated prediction promotes itself into evidence.

---

## T2 — Deterministic transition

Hold constant:

- state generation;
- semantic coordinate;
- intervention;
- recipe ordinal;
- bound context;
- allowed RNG state, if any.

Run the transition repeatedly.

### Expected

~~~text
transition_1 == transition_2 == ... == transition_n
~~~

bit-for-bit for an exact deterministic recipe.

### Falsifies

A replayable recipe whose result changes without an admitted input changing.

---

## T3 — Intervention branching

Given one bound state:

~~~text
do(A) -> branch_A
do(B) -> branch_B
~~~

where A and B are known to affect the modeled mechanism differently.

### Expected

The branches remain separately addressable and replayable.

### Falsifies

A transition API in which intervention identity is lost after lowering.

---

## T4 — Parent immutability

Create several counterfactual branches from generation G.

### Expected

After all virtual rollouts:

~~~text
hash(G_after) == hash(G_before)
~~~

unless an explicit observation/revision/commit step was performed.

### Falsifies

Simulation that contaminates the state from which the counterfactual was defined.

---

## T5 — Observational equivalence is not causal equivalence

Construct two models with the same observational correlation but different causal structure, for example:

~~~text
A <- C -> B

versus

A -> B
~~~

Choose an intervention that distinguishes them.

### Expected

Passive observation may be indistinguishable.

The discriminating intervention must produce different predicted consequences.

### Falsifies

A causal layer that is only relabeled correlation.

---

## T6 — Repetition cannot promote epistemic class

Replay the same prediction one million times without new external evidence.

### Expected

~~~text
epistemic_license_after == epistemic_license_before
~~~

except for explicitly separate reproducibility metadata.

### Falsifies

A system in which internal repetition is mistaken for independent empirical support.

---

## T7 — Causal composition floor

Given:

~~~text
A -> B
B -> C
~~~

with different epistemic licenses on the two links, infer A -> C.

### Expected

The composed result cannot acquire a stronger causal license than permitted by the weakest required support and composition rule.

### Falsifies

Composition that manufactures epistemic strength.

---

## T8 — No-materialization rollout

Run N consecutive internal transitions.

### Expected

No full intermediate population is allocated unless an explicit observation/debug/commit boundary requests it.

### Falsifies

A world-model path that reconstructs/materializes complete state between every transition despite no consumer requiring it.

---

## T9 — Relevant intervention changes the witness

Take a deterministic ReplayKey and change exactly one intervention coordinate known to affect the transition.

### Expected

The transition witness or predicted delta changes.

Then perturb an explicitly irrelevant coordinate.

### Expected

The witness remains unchanged.

### Falsifies

Either missing causal dependence or accidental dependence on irrelevant state.

---

## T10 — Epistemic acquisition beats confirmation

Create two live hypotheses H1 and H2 and two possible experiments:

~~~text
E_confirm
    likely confirms the currently dominant hypothesis
    but yields nearly the same outcome under H1 and H2

E_split
    yields strongly different outcomes under H1 and H2
~~~

### Expected

An information-seeking scheduler ranks E_split higher when the objective is uncertainty reduction.

### Falsifies

A planner that claims epistemic exploration but only maximizes current-belief reward/confidence.

---

## T11 — Observation discrepancy reaches revision

Predict outcome P, then inject external observation O where:

~~~text
P != O
~~~

### Expected

The discrepancy becomes an explicit revision input.

It must not be:

- silently overwritten by P;
- discarded because P had high confidence;
- folded into the virtual branch without provenance.

### Falsifies

A closed-loop architecture that cannot learn from being wrong.

---

## T12 — Causal-band falsifier

Provide an arbitrarily large purely observational dataset with strong agreement and correlation but no intervention/counterfactual evidence required by the highest causal tier.

### Expected

The claim cannot reach that causal tier.

### Falsifies

Any mapping in which sample count, confidence, or agreement alone can manufacture causal license.

This is likely the most important direct test for the current 61..63 reasoning-band discussion.

---

## T13 — Branch contamination test

From the same parent G:

1. simulate do(A);
2. discard the branch;
3. simulate do(B).

Compare with:

1. start again from pristine G;
2. simulate do(B).

### Expected

Both do(B) results are identical.

### Falsifies

Hidden state leakage between counterfactual branches.

---

## T14 — Materialization equivalence

Execute the same transition chain in two modes:

~~~text
Mode 1:
materialize every intermediate state

Mode 2:
keep intermediate state implicit and materialize only the final boundary
~~~

### Expected

The final observable/certified result is identical.

### Falsifies

An implicit fold path that changes semantics relative to the explicit reference path.

This is the direct test of the substrate's proposed alternative to repeated latent decode/reconstruction.

---

## T15 — Failure is informative

Construct two hypotheses whose predictions differ only under one intervention. Run that intervention and return an observation that falsifies the currently dominant hypothesis.

### Expected

Revision decreases support for the falsified hypothesis and preserves the observation as provenance-bearing evidence.

### Falsifies

A scheduler/revision loop that treats failed predictions only as execution errors rather than scientific evidence.

---

# Minimal substrate experiment

Do not build a WorldModel subsystem.

Start with one bounded experiment over existing machinery:

~~~text
1. bind one deterministic parent generation G
2. choose one coordinate with two legal interventions A and B
3. lower each intervention through an existing recipe/fold path
4. keep both results virtual
5. prove that G is unchanged
6. prove deterministic replay for A and B
7. materialize only the terminal predicted observables
8. inject one external observation
9. route only that observation through revision
10. prove that replay count alone never changes epistemic license
~~~

Success criterion:

> **The substrate can simulate two intervention-conditioned futures from one immutable state and can revise only from externally admitted evidence.**

If this works, the core world-model contract exists without introducing a large new abstraction.

# What not to import

The paper does **not** justify:

- making every substrate state a neural embedding;
- treating prediction confidence as causal evidence;
- adding a monolithic WorldModel service;
- materializing every simulated future;
- allowing simulations to mutate certified state;
- promoting a claim because many internal rollouts agree;
- collapsing observation, intervention, and counterfactual evidence into one scalar.

The useful import is the contract between those things, not a prescribed implementation.

---

# Strong falsifier

The harvest fails if existing lance-graph primitives cannot satisfy this invariant without a new stateful subsystem:

> **From one immutable bound generation, two different interventions can be replayed into distinct virtual futures; neither future can certify itself; an external observation can revise the certified state; and the result is invariant between eager and lazy materialization.**

If that cannot be expressed cleanly with the existing fold/mask/generation/revision machinery, then the proposed “world model as algebra” reading is too optimistic and a new primitive may be justified.

Until then:

> **Do not build a world-model object. Prove that the substrate already behaves like one.**
