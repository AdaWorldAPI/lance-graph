# Wang 1993 / arXiv:1303.1517 — Belief revision is not merely Bayesian conditioning

**Paper:** Pei Wang, *Belief Revision in Probability Theory*, UAI 1993; archived on arXiv 2013.  
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.1517  
**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.1517  
**Read:** 2026-10-11.

## Punchline

> **Updating probabilities within a fixed model and revising the evidence supporting a belief are different operations. A probability assignment alone does not tell a reasoning system where its evidential support came from or whether a new observation is independent.**

## Paper argument

Wang examines Bayes' rule and Jeffrey-like probability updates and argues against treating them as universally general *belief revision* rules. In a probability-based system:
- **Propagation** may derive probability values for new propositions from current assumptions;
- **Revision** may change the assigned certainty of an existing proposition based on a newly incorporated source of evidence;
- **Updating** may change distributions within a particular conditioning/model framework.

The paper's critique is **conditional on the missing meta-information**: a probability distribution alone may not encode the evidence dependencies necessary for general revision. Do not turn this into the overbroad claim that Bayesian statistics never learns from data or that Bayesian belief revision is impossible in all richer probabilistic frameworks.

## Why it matters for our architecture

A signed/unsigned F/C byte pair is not an evidence dependency graph. Neither are log addresses alone if two addresses carry derivations of the same observation.

`
observation root R
 ├─ derivation A (event ea)
 └─ derivation B (event eb)
        └─ merge(A,B)   <-- don't add R twice
`

Separation:
- **Observation:** new evidence root if truly independent.
- **Deduction:** propagated support; preserve roots.
- **Revision:** merge only admissible independent evidence and retain lineage.
- **Correction:** retract/disqualify a root, recompute dependent conclusions.
- **Intervention:** Pearl do() changes a causal world/model, not merely a belief's numeric confidence.

## Falsifier suite

1. Same numeric prior belief generated from **two distinct histories**; present the same additional evidence. Confirm result may differ when independence relationships differ.
2. Duplicate an observation through two chains; replay does not mint new evidence.
3. Retract a previously trusted observation and recompute the dependency cone; no orphans with inflated c.
4. Change conditioning assumptions with no new observation; classify as model update/propagation, not independent evidence addition.
5. Maintain time/version semantics for historical replay. Reprocessing old records under a new semantic reading needs explicit migration, not silent reinterpretation.
6. Track error class: inference-wrong, source-overlap, bad mapping, authorization-denied, unknown.

## Limits

This is a **philosophical/formal critique** of probability-only revision, not a benchmark proving NARS beats contemporary Bayesian networks. The project mapping, root identity, replay gates and tests above are hypotheses based on the conceptual distinction.
