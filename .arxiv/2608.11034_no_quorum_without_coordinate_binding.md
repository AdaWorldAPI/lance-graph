# SCOUT — No Quorum Without Coordinate Binding

**Paper:** arXiv:2608.11034  
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11034  
**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11034

## Punchline

> **Consensus is only meaningful after equivalence has been bound. A strict majority is evidence about agreement, not a truth oracle; disagreement without a majority must remain explicitly inconclusive.**

SCOUT is useful to lance-graph less as a distributed-training diagnostic than as a compact recipe for turning redundant observations into replayable evidence:

```text
bind comparable population
        ↓
produce compact evidence
        ↓
fold to winner / support
        ↓
strict-majority guard
   ┌────┴─────────────┐
majority          no majority
   ↓                  ↓
optional mask     INCONCLUSIVE
   ↓
attributed outliers
```

The architectural harvest is small: **Bind → Fold → Guard → optional Mask → Replay/Revision**.

No new consensus subsystem is required until existing fold/mask machinery is proven insufficient.

---

## What the paper actually establishes

### 1. Consensus requires equivalent replicas

SCOUT does not vote across arbitrary ranks. It first constructs peer groups that are expected to perform equivalent work, matching the relevant model-parallel coordinates and aligning input, training coordinate, and randomness.

Portable rule:

> **No quorum without coordinate binding.**

A vote across semantically heterogeneous observations is not weak evidence; it is a malformed comparison.

For lance-graph:

```text
same semantic coordinate
+ same recipe/operator
+ same admissible evidence domain
+ same relevant generation/state
────────────────────────────────
equivalence class
        ↓
agreement fold
```

### 2. Exact consensus is Fold → Guard → Mask

For deterministic evidence values `E`, SCOUT selects the most frequent value and count:

```text
(winner, support) = mode(E)
```

If:

```text
support <= population / 2
```

the result is **Inconclusive**.

Only with a strict majority does it mark observations that differ from the winner.

That is naturally expressible as:

```text
population
   ↓
fold(signature -> count)
   ↓
argmax
   ↓
support > N/2 ?
   ├─ no  → Inconclusive
   └─ yes → winner
              ↓
          optional mask(signature != winner)
```

The outlier bitmap is therefore a derived product, not the primary computation.

### 3. Agreement is evidence, not truth

The paper explicitly limits exact consensus to sparse-fault attribution. It is **not** Byzantine agreement and not a correctness oracle.

A faulty value held by a strict majority wins. A common-mode error shared by every peer is invisible.

Portable invariant:

```text
Agree != True
Attributed != False
Inconclusive != Unknown forever
```

Instead:

```text
Agree        = current comparable evidence closes
Attributed   = a strict-majority reference localizes disagreement
Inconclusive = disagreement exists but attribution is unjustified
```

For lance-graph, accumulated agreement/disagreement may become an evidence field, but it must remain distinct from causal or epistemic grading.

### 4. Inconclusive is a first-class scheduling signal

SCOUT refuses to name a culprit when peers disagree but no strict majority exists. In a two-peer disagreement, for example, attribution is impossible.

This is valuable beyond fault diagnosis:

```text
Agree
  → cheap continuation

Attributed
  → inspect/replay localized minority

Inconclusive
  → epistemic pothole
  → allocate exploration / gather independent evidence
```

A deterministic reasoning substrate should prefer an explicit unresolved state over fabricated certainty.

### 5. Compact signatures preserve comparison without moving full state

SCOUT folds large tensors into deterministic, position-sensitive signatures and exchanges compact evidence rather than full tensors. Its implementation currently uses 64-bit signatures.

Transferable rule:

> **Compare compact witnesses first; materialize full state only when the verdict requires it.**

For lance-graph this aligns with:

```text
fold first
mask second
materialize last
```

and with the wider goal of keeping large populations implicit until a breaking point needs inspection.

### 6. Replay converts a suspicion into an experiment

For straggler and silent-data-corruption diagnosis, SCOUT replays selected work beside the live job while preserving relevant model state, kernels, allocations, communication conditions, and operating pressure.

To make numerical comparison meaningful it aligns inputs, RNG state, and deterministic execution.

Portable contract:

```text
same coordinate
+ same state generation
+ same input
+ same recipe
+ same randomness contract
──────────────────────────
same deterministic witness
```

A divergence under those conditions is much stronger evidence than a historical correlation.

For 0..63 reasoning recipes, this suggests that the recipe ordinal can double as part of a **reproduction coordinate** for deterministic thought experiments.

### 7. Coverage can be represented by execution-equivalent exemplars

SCOUT does not replay every observed MoE shape. It selects representatives that cover recurring execution paths.

In its reported experiments, examples include:

| Admitted shapes | Selected representatives | Reduction |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 3,457 | 16 | 99.54% |
| 2,048 | 10 | 99.51% |
| 1,024 | 18 | 98.24% |
| 2,048 | 19 | 99.07% |

For grouped kernels, 2,048 admitted shapes were reduced to 18–48 representatives depending on experts per kernel, corresponding to roughly 97.66–99.12% reduction.

The transferable idea is not the specific numbers. It is:

```text
state space
    ↓
execution-equivalence classes
    ↓
representatives
    ↓
coverage replay
```

This is directly relevant to Rubikon/batchwriter reasoning: a million potential fold/reasoning operations need not imply a million separately materialized replay states.

### 8. Certification belongs before commitment

SCOUT uses clean replay coverage to certify checkpoint candidates. A checkpoint whose numerical integrity has not been verified is not treated as interchangeable with a verified one.

Portable ordering:

```text
compute / reason
      ↓
replay or verify
      ↓
certify
      ↓
commit / materialize
```

rather than:

```text
compute
  ↓
commit
  ↓
discover corruption later
```

For lance-graph this maps naturally onto generation boundaries and selective materialization.

# Harvest for lance-graph

## H1 — Bind before Fold

Consensus needs an explicit comparison scope.

Possible conceptual key:

```text
ConsensusScope {
    semantic_coordinate,
    recipe,
    evidence_domain,
    generation,
}
```

This does not imply a new storage type. It may remain a logical contract over existing coordinates and masks.

Hard invariant:

> **Two observations that differ on a coordinate relevant to the claimed equivalence must never contribute to the same quorum.**

---

## H2 — Keep the minimal verdict algebra

The useful three-state result is:

```text
Agree
Attributed
Inconclusive
```

A minimal derived view could conceptually expose:

```text
Consensus {
    winner,
    support,
    population,
    status,
}
```

with:

```text
outliers() -> Mask
```

only when attribution is actually consumed.

Do not bake full evidence vectors or materialized bitmaps into the primary contract unless a consumer proves the need.

---

## H3 — Agreement fields may hydrate ontology populations

For a bound ontology slice:

```text
class     = Mammal
predicate = warm_blooded
recipe    = observational/property-check
```

a fold can accumulate:

```text
agree
disagree
unknown/inapplicable
```

without asserting that the majority value is truth.

This gives a deterministic **agreement field** over hydrated ontology populations while leaving provenance and causality as separate dimensions.

---

## H4 — Keep consensus below causal grading

A useful separation for the existing reasoning frontier is:

```text
agreement / disagreement
        ↓
replayable evidence
        ↓
provenance / independence
        ↓
counterfactual and causal tests
        ↓
higher epistemic grade
```

SCOUT supplies an agreement/localization mechanism, not a justification for collapsing agreement into bits 61..63 or treating it as causality.

---

## H5 — Inconclusive should feed Rubikon

Treat lack of majority as a scheduling condition:

```text
fold evidence
    ↓
strict-majority guard
    ├─ closes        → continue cheaply
    ├─ minority      → targeted replay
    └─ inconclusive  → frontier exploration
```

This is an attractive bridge from deterministic fold machinery to selective epistemic exploration.

---

## H6 — Replay identity should include recipe identity

For deterministic reasoning, define replay equality over the inputs that are semantically allowed to affect the result.

Conceptually:

```text
ReplayKey =
    coordinate
  × generation
  × recipe_ordinal
  × bound evidence inputs
  × randomness contract
```

Then:

```text
same ReplayKey -> same witness
```

becomes a falsifiable substrate invariant.

# Testables

## T1 — Strict-majority attribution

### Input

```text
[A, A, A, B, C]
```

### Expected

```text
winner     = A
support    = 3
population = 5
status     = Attributed
outliers   = {3,4}
```

### Falsifies

A consensus fold that cannot localize all minority values once a strict majority exists.

---

## T2 — No fabricated winner

### Input

```text
[A, A, B, B]
```

### Expected

```text
status   = Inconclusive
outliers = empty / not materialized
```

No tie-breaker may manufacture a semantic winner.

### Falsifies

Any implementation that converts absence of evidence into arbitrary attribution.

---

## T3 — Two-peer disagreement stays group-scoped

### Input

```text
[A, B]
```

### Expected

Disagreement may be detected, but neither member can be named as the faulty/outlier observation solely from consensus.

### Falsifies

Pairwise disagreement being misrepresented as directional evidence.

---

## T4 — Equivalence binding guard

Create two observations that agree in value but differ on a coordinate relevant to the recipe, for example:

```text
same value
different semantic_coordinate
```

or:

```text
same coordinate
different generation
```

### Expected

They cannot enter the same quorum.

### Falsifies

Consensus whose apparent strength comes from mixing incomparable populations.

---

## T5 — Common-mode falsifier

### Input

```text
[WRONG, WRONG, WRONG]
```

### Expected

The consensus layer may legitimately return:

```text
Agree
```

The test documentation must explicitly state that this does **not** prove truth.

### Falsifies

Any architecture or API that promises correctness from agreement alone.

---

## T6 — Fold before Mask

For a population with a strict majority, compute:

```text
winner
support
population
status
```

without allocating an outlier bitmap.

Only then request:

```text
outliers()
```

### Expected

The first phase allocates no population-sized attribution object; the second produces exactly the minority mask.

### Falsifies

A consensus implementation that forces bitmap materialization merely to learn whether a majority exists.

---

## T7 — Deterministic replay

Hold constant:

- semantic coordinate;
- generation/state;
- recipe ordinal;
- bound inputs;
- RNG state when applicable.

Run the same recipe repeatedly.

### Expected

```text
witness_1 == witness_2 == ... == witness_n
```

bit-for-bit for an exact-comparison recipe.

### Falsifies

A supposedly replayable reasoning path whose output changes without an allowed input changing.

---

## T8 — Relevant-bit divergence

Start from T7, then perturb exactly one input/state component declared relevant by the recipe.

### Expected

Either:

1. the witness changes, or
2. the recipe contract explicitly proves that the perturbation is semantically irrelevant.

Silent dependence gaps are failures.

### Falsifies

Replay keys that omit state capable of changing the result.

---

## T9 — Inconclusive becomes schedulable

Feed a no-majority population into the reasoning scheduler.

### Expected

The result is represented as an explicit unresolved condition suitable for targeted evidence acquisition or replay.

It must not silently:

- choose a winner;
- convert to generic failure;
- disappear as an empty mask.

### Falsifies

A pipeline that loses epistemic uncertainty at the boundary between fold and orchestration.

---

## T10 — Provenance independence

Construct:

```text
source S
 ├─ copy A
 ├─ copy B
 └─ copy C
```

where A/B/C repeat the same underlying claim.

Compare against three genuinely independent witnesses.

### Expected

Three copies of one provenance root must not automatically count as three independent epistemic witnesses.

### Falsifies

Ontology hydration that turns duplicated source lineage into artificial confidence.

This is a lance-graph extension beyond SCOUT, but necessary if agreement is reused for knowledge/reasoning rather than machine-fault localization.

---

## T11 — Representative replay coverage

Create many states that map onto a smaller set of known execution recipes.

### Expected

A representative set covers all declared execution classes:

```text
coverage(representatives) == coverage(full_catalog)
```

while:

```text
|representatives| << |full_catalog|
```

### Strong falsifier

Inject one new execution path not represented by the catalog.

The coverage test must fail rather than silently certify it.

### Falsifies

Compression that preserves count reduction but loses behavioral coverage.

---

## T12 — Certification gate

Create three generations:

```text
G0 verified
G1 unverified
G2 verified-corrupt / replay-failing
```

### Expected

A consumer requesting a certified recovery/materialization boundary may select G0 but not G1 or G2.

### Falsifies

Generation logic that confuses recency with verified integrity.

---

# Minimal substrate experiment

No new storage primitive is needed.

Construct a synthetic population with:

```text
coordinate
recipe
generation
signature
provenance_root
```

Run these cases:

```text
1. unanimous equivalent population
2. strict majority + one minority
3. strict majority + several distinct minorities
4. exact tie
5. two-peer disagreement
6. same values but mismatched coordinates
7. common-mode wrong value
8. duplicate-provenance majority
9. deterministic replay
10. one uncovered execution class
```

Measure:

- winner ordinal/signature;
- support count;
- population count;
- verdict;
- whether a mask was materialized;
- outlier popcount when requested;
- replay identity;
- coverage completeness;
- provenance independence.

Minimum success pattern:

```text
equivalent unanimous      -> Agree
strict majority           -> Attributed
tie                       -> Inconclusive
two-peer disagreement     -> Inconclusive
coordinate mismatch       -> rejected before quorum
common-mode wrong         -> Agree, but never Truth
duplicate provenance      -> independence warning/failure
same ReplayKey            -> bit-identical witness
uncovered execution class -> certification fails
```

# Guardrails

## Do not equate majority with truth

SCOUT assumes the healthy value is held by a strict majority. That operational assumption is suitable for sparse machine faults, but knowledge reasoning needs additional provenance, independence, counterfactual, observational, or mathematical constraints.

## Do not vote before binding comparability

The majority operator is downstream of equivalence. Reversing that order creates meaningless confidence.

## Do not materialize disagreement eagerly

Winner/support/status are scalar fold results. A full outlier mask is a downstream diagnostic view.

## Do not hide inconclusive behind an empty bitmap

SCOUT keeps an explicit status precisely because `B = 0` can mean either complete agreement or inability to attribute.

## Do not over-generalize the reported replay compression

The coverage reductions are workload- and kernel-specific results from the paper's evaluated MoE shapes. The transferable idea is execution-equivalence compression, not a guaranteed 97–99% reduction for lance-graph.

## Do not over-generalize evaluation scale

The paper's fault-injection evaluation is small relative to production frontier training: at most 16 A100 GPUs across two hosts. Production-scale false-positive, detection-accuracy, overhead, and recovery-savings behavior remains future work.

# Architecture consequence

The clean harvest is a narrow invariant chain:

```text
semantic population
      ↓
BIND equivalence
      ↓
FOLD compact evidence
      ↓
strict-majority GUARD
      ├─ Agree
      ├─ Attributed ──> optional MASK
      └─ Inconclusive ─> epistemic scheduling
      ↓
REPLAY when needed
      ↓
CERTIFY
      ↓
MATERIALIZE / COMMIT
```

For the wider substrate:

```text
0..63 recipe
    ×
bound coordinate
    ×
local/global evidence population
    ×
folded agreement
    ×
provenance independence
    ×
replay
    ×
revision
```

The important move is not to make consensus smarter.

It is to make **comparison admissibility, disagreement, and refusal to decide** deterministic and replayable.

That is the piece worth carrying into lance-graph.
