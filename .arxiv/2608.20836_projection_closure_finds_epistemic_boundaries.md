# Cross-Observable Reconstruction — Projection Closure Finds Epistemic Boundaries

**Paper:** arXiv:2608.20836  
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20836  
**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20836

## Punchline

> **When one observable can reconstruct another almost as well as the target reconstructs itself, the two views share usable latent structure. Where that cross-view closure breaks while self-reconstruction remains good, the residual localizes genuinely new information.**

The paper is useful to lance-graph less as an MLP recipe than as an operational test for shared information and epistemic boundaries.

Rather than asking only whether two observables are correlated, compare cross-reconstruction against the target's own reconstruction floor.

~~~
A -> B_hat
B -> B_hat_self
~~~

A small excess error means that A carries much of B's recoverable structure. A localized increase in excess error means the previously redundant views no longer close over one another.

---

## What the paper actually establishes

The paper studies cross-reconstruction among observables of the 2D Fermi-Hubbard model, including total density N, double occupancy D, and spin-spin correlation S.

### 1. Cross-reconstruction can approach self-reconstruction

Reported cross/self MSE ratios include:

| Mapping | MSE relative to target self-reconstruction |
|---|---:|
| S -> N | ~1.13x |
| D -> S | ~1.24x |
| D -> N | ~1.35x |
| N -> S | ~1.72x |
| S -> D | ~1.91x |
| N -> D | ~1.99x |

Portable quantity:

~~~
ProjectionClosure(A -> B)
    = E(A -> B) / E(B -> B)
~~~

This is more informative than raw prediction error because B -> B estimates how difficult B is to reconstruct at all.

### 2. Extra cross-view error localizes regime changes

Define:

~~~
EpistemicResidual(A,B)
    = E(A -> B) - E(B -> B)
~~~

In smooth regions, cross-reconstruction stays close to self-reconstruction. Around physically interesting crossover/transition regions, excess cross-error grows locally.

That distinguishes:

~~~
B is intrinsically difficult
~~~

from:

~~~
A specifically stopped carrying information that B still contains
~~~

This is an operational projection-closure failure.

### 3. Correct semantic pairing matters

The shuffle control preserves source statistics/local structure while destroying the physically correct source-target pairing. Cross-reconstruction degrades strongly.

Portable rule:

> **A claimed semantic relation should fail when its binding is shuffled while lower-order statistics are preserved.**

If it does not, the model may be exploiting layout, density, coordinates, frequency, or histogram rather than the claimed relation.

### 4. Useful structure is locally smooth rather than bit-fragile

The model uses each point together with its eight neighbors in a 3x3 neighborhood over the phase-diagram grid.

Important qualification:

> The Moore-8 neighborhood is over sampled phase-diagram coordinates, not the physical Hubbard lattice itself.

Noise injection degrades reconstruction gradually rather than causing immediate collapse.

Transferable lesson:

> **A relation supported by distributed local structure should survive moderate perturbation better than a lookup-table or exact-coordinate shortcut.**

### 5. The learned local relation transfers beyond the training plaquette size

For N -> S, reported cross/self ratios remain in the same broad regime when evaluated on larger geometries:

~~~
2x2 : ~1.72
2x3 : ~1.624
2x4 : ~1.769
3x3 : ~1.863
~~~

This does not prove scale invariance. It gives a useful test shape:

> **If a mechanism is genuinely local and compositional, modest domain growth should degrade gracefully rather than discontinuously.**

# Harvest for lance-graph

## H1 — Projection Closure

For semantic views A and B:

~~~
ProjectionClosure(A -> B)
    = E(A -> B) / E(B -> B)
~~~

Interpretation:

~~~
~1.0   A contains nearly all recoverable information needed for B
>1.0   A is missing some B-specific information
large  A and B do not share enough usable structure for this reconstruction
~~~

Candidate uses:

- SPO/FC projections;
- ontology view vs observed evidence;
- codebook view vs raw feature view;
- parent/child ontology slices;
- local Moore context vs global population;
- correlation view vs causal/revision view;
- one reasoning-band projection vs another.

Do not assume symmetry:

~~~
Closure(A -> B) != Closure(B -> A)
~~~

Directional asymmetry may itself be informative.

## H2 — Epistemic Residual

Use either:

~~~
EpistemicResidual(A,B)
    = E(A -> B) - E(B -> B)
~~~

or normalized:

~~~
RelativeResidual(A,B)
    = E(A -> B) / E(B -> B) - 1
~~~

The useful signal is not merely "prediction is hard."

It is:

> **The current view should have predicted this other view, but specifically failed to do so.**

Candidate triggers:

- missing-link search;
- counterfactual exploration;
- revision;
- hypothesis branching;
- selective materialization;
- local plasticity;
- increased reasoning-band allocation.

## H3 — Sharpen 59/60 into closure vs residual

Possible interpretation of the existing 59/60 frontier:

~~~
59  expected closure / predicted completion
60  structured residual / unresolved closure failure
~~~

Not storage semantics and not yet an implementation contract.

Architectural separation:

~~~
0..58   deterministic operators attempt known reconstruction
59      expected closure exists
60      observed evidence violates that closure
61..63  grade what kind of relation/explanation resolves the violation
~~~

Compactly:

> **59/60 discover that the Sudoku no longer closes. 61..63 grade the epistemic quality of the explanation.**

A closure failure must not automatically imply causality.

## H4 — Semantic shuffle becomes a hard substrate falsifier

For any proposed relation A -> B:

1. preserve source and target populations;
2. preserve value distributions;
3. preserve local topology where feasible;
4. destroy only semantic pairing;
5. replay the same reconstruction/fold.

Expected:

~~~
real binding      -> closure
shuffled binding  -> closure collapses
~~~

If the score barely changes, the system may be exploiting coordinates, density, ordering, frequency, histogram, or topology alone.

## H5 — Residuals should be structured, not merely large

Desired lifecycle:

~~~
low residual in known/stable regions
        |
        v
localized structured residual
        |
        v
new relation / boundary / missing edge / regime change
        |
        v
validated repair
        |
        v
closure restored
~~~

The restoration step is essential. Otherwise novelty detection never becomes learning.

# Testables

## T1 — Cross/Self Ratio

### Hypothesis

Known related views should reconstruct one another substantially better than unrelated controls after normalization by self-reconstruction.

### Measure

~~~
R(A -> B) = E(A -> B) / E(B -> B)
~~~

### Pass shape

~~~
R(related A -> B) << R(unrelated C -> B)
~~~

### Falsifies

Claims of shared substrate that do not carry reconstructable information.

---

## T2 — Semantic Binding Shuffle

### Procedure

Shuffle source-target binding while preserving as much lower-order structure as possible.

Preserve:

- source values;
- target values;
- marginal distributions;
- population size;
- local-neighborhood statistics if feasible.

Destroy:

- actual semantic correspondence.

### Expected

~~~
R(shuffled A -> B) >> R(real A -> B)
~~~

### Falsifies

Layout, frequency, histogram, or coordinate shortcuts masquerading as semantic coupling.

---

## T3 — Coordinate-Only Baseline

Compare:

~~~
coordinates/context -> B
A + coordinates/context -> B
A alone -> B
~~~

### Required result

~~~
E(A + context -> B) < E(context -> B)
~~~

by a meaningful margin.

Otherwise apparent cross-observable reconstruction may mostly be phase-position interpolation.

### Falsifies

"Shared information" claims where A adds little beyond explicit coordinates/context.

---

## T4 — Noise Sweep

Inject controlled perturbation into A, for example:

~~~
sigma = 0
        0.05
        0.10
        0.20
        0.30
~~~

### Expected

Graceful degradation:

~~~
noise up
  ->
cross/self ratio worsens smoothly
~~~

### Falsifies

Brittle lookup behavior or dependence on exact bit patterns where a distributed relation was claimed.

---

## T5 — Residual Localization

For samples with known transitions, ontology boundaries, contradictory evidence, or injected missing links, map:

~~~
DeltaE(A,B)
~~~

over the domain.

### Expected

Residual concentrates near the real epistemic boundary.

### Stronger replay test

~~~
before repair: structured residual
after repair:  residual contracts
~~~

### Falsifies

A novelty score that is merely generic entropy or model difficulty.

---

## T6 — Neighborhood Ablation

Compare:

~~~
self only
self + cardinal neighbors
self + Moore-8
larger neighborhood
global context
~~~

### Question

Does local context provide real additional closure, and where does it saturate?

### Expected

If local plasticity matters:

~~~
error(self + useful local neighborhood)
    <
error(self only)
~~~

without requiring global materialization.

### Falsifies

Claims that local interaction is necessary when the same result comes from the center coordinate alone.

---

## T7 — Scale / Domain Transfer

Calibrate on a smaller domain and replay the same rule on a larger domain without retraining or changing semantics.

Candidate lance-graph shapes:

- 8k -> 64k population;
- one ontology subtree -> larger sibling set;
- one tile -> multiple tiles;
- one local Sudoku region -> larger board;
- one tenant slice -> multi-tenant synthetic domain.

### Expected

~~~
domain size up
  ->
closure degrades gradually
~~~

rather than discontinuously.

### Falsifies

A supposedly local/compositional rule that actually memorizes finite training geometry.

# Minimal substrate experiment

A first experiment does not need an MLP.

Use an existing deterministic fold/prediction mechanism.

Construct three views of the same synthetic population:

~~~
A = source evidence
B = deterministic target relation
C = unrelated control
~~~

Run:

~~~
A -> B
B -> B
C -> B
shuffle(A) -> B
noise(A) -> B
A -> B with one structured missing relation inserted
~~~

Record:

- cross/self ratio;
- absolute residual;
- residual locality;
- popcount of violated cells;
- replay stability.

Minimum success pattern:

~~~
R(A -> B)            low
R(C -> B)            high
R(shuffled A -> B)   high
noise sweep          graceful
inserted gap         localized residual
repair gap           residual collapses
replay               bit-identical
~~~

No new storage primitive is required to test this.

# Guardrails

## Do not conclude that cross-reconstruction proves causality

A predicts B does not imply A causes B.

The paper establishes shared recoverable structure, not causal direction.

That is precisely why a 59/60 closure frontier should remain below any 61..63 causal grading.

## Do not equate Moore-8 with the physical Hubbard lattice

The 3x3 neighborhood is over phase-diagram sample coordinates.

The transferable principle is local-context reconstruction, not validation of a specific spatial substrate.

## Do not use raw entropy as a substitute for closure failure

High entropy may occur in a difficult but already understood region.

Projection closure asks a sharper question:

> Given what this view normally tells us about the other view, what specifically failed to reconstruct?

## Do not accept reconstruction without shortcut controls

At minimum compare against:

- self-reconstruction;
- coordinate/context-only baseline;
- shuffled semantic binding;
- unrelated-view baseline.

Without those controls, apparent shared structure may be interpolation.

# Architecture consequence

The clean harvest is a three-stage reasoning invariant:

~~~
known relation
    |
    v
predict another projection
    |
    v
compare against observation
    |
    +-- closes ----------> cheap replay / no escalation
    |
    +-- structured miss --> epistemic residual
                              |
                              v
                    hypothesis / revision /
                    missing-link exploration
                              |
                              v
                        validated repair
                              |
                              v
                         closure restored
~~~

In compact form:

~~~
Projection Closure
    = what current knowledge can mutually reconstruct

Epistemic Residual
    = where that mutual reconstruction specifically fails

Learning
    = restoring closure without destroying replayability
~~~

That is the part worth carrying into lance-graph.
