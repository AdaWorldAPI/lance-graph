# 2026-10-20 — D-RU-1 (U1): a JC statistics batch over shared power-sum passes

**Status:** MEASURED (lab probe `crates/jc-batch-probe`). Plan: `.claude/plans/d-relalg-unified-v1.md` §3 and the stage table (U1).

## What landed

- `answer_separately`: each request evaluates its relalg selection into a keep mask, then runs one power or cross pass over it. This is the baseline.
- `answer_batched`: requests are reduced to the distinct selections after `relalg::normalize`. For each selection, each distinct unordered lane pair gets one `GroupCrossPowerSumsI32` pass keyed by the group lane. A lane that no pair covers gets one `GroupPowerSumsI32` pass.
  - An ungrouped answer merges the groups with `checked_merge`.
  - A univariate answer reads the `x` or `y` side of the cross sums.
  - A reversed pair swaps the sides.
- Statistics: mean, variance, one-way ANOVA, covariance, Pearson, R², and the simple regression line in both directions.
- Both paths refuse a request built for another version (`BatchError::VersionMismatch`).
- `Cost` is counted from the programs that actually run: programs, selection passes, sum passes, rows traversed, values read, mask bytes.
- `examples/bench.rs` (`BENCH_N`, `BENCH_REPS`).

## Measured

Battery: 252 requests over 2 selections and 7 `I32` lanes. It reduces to 2 selection passes and 42 cross passes; no univariate pass is needed. The table gives medians of 5 warm runs on 4 cores, load below 1.

| rows | separate ms | batched ms | time | rows traversed | values read | mask bytes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100,000 | 40.2 | 5.8 | 6.9× | 11.5× | 8.5× | 126× |
| 1,000,000 | 424.5 | 66.2 | 6.4× | 11.5× | 8.5× | 126× |
| 4,000,000 | 1,818.9 | 279.1 | 6.5× | 11.5× | 8.5× | 126× |

At 1M rows the counts are:
- programs: 504 → 44;
- rows traversed: 504M → 44M;
- values read: 1,092M → 129M;
- mask bytes: 31.5 MB → 250 kB.

The time ratio is below the values-read ratio because a cross pass folds five sums per row.

**Correctness.** The batched answers equal the separate answers bit for bit. They also agree with an independent f64 oracle over directly selected rows: JC's `pearson` and `anova_one_way`, and two-pass mean and variance.

**Integer range.**
- At `i32::MIN`/`i32::MAX` values, `Σx²` passes `u64` and `Σxy` passes `i64`; the test asserts both. Mean and variance still equal the exact `i128` values.
- At an offset of 10⁹, the naive one-pass f64 Pearson is off by more than 10⁻³. The exact path is within 10⁻¹² of the two-pass value.

**Tests and disables.** 8 tests pass. Six disable runs each turned red, with every disable patch's anchor asserted:
- swap the reversed pair;
- read the `y` side as `x`;
- drop the pair dedup;
- skip `normalize`;
- merge only the first group;
- drop the version check.

## OPEN

- No R reference: R is not installed in this container. The reference is JC's slice functions plus exact integer sums.
- A merge overflow cannot happen below the 2³²-row cap the terminals enforce, so `MergeOverflow` is untested; the cap itself is mask-risc's test.
- The timing of one battery shape. Batches with few requests per pair, so less sharing, are not measured, and neither is the break-even request count.
- Selections are shared only when they are equal after normalization; overlapping selections (U2's let-planes) are not shared yet.
