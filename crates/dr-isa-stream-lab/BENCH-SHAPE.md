# dr-isa-stream-lab: DT64-shaped 64×64 and 256×256 BF16 objects

D-DT64-GEMM-SHAPE-1 (64×64) and -2 (256×256). Generated by
`cargo run --release --bin shape`; raw output of three runs is in
`results/shape-2026-11-10-run{1,2,3}.txt`. Before timing and again after the
timed runs, every arm is checked against the scalar oracle. Allocations and
peak temporary bytes come from a counting global allocator.

**Host.** Intel Xeon @ 2.10 GHz VM (AMX-BF16, AVX-512-BF16), 4 vCPU,
L1d 48 KiB, L2 2 MiB, L3 260 MiB (as reported by the guest). Tile tier
`AMX TDPBF16PS`, single-threaded, rustc 1.99.0. The numbers are not from
dedicated silicon. They moved by up to about 35% between runs with the guest
idle, so differences under that size are not findings.

## Shape

```
universe (65,536 identities x F property lanes, source-owned slab)
  -> WHERE (region or scattered mask, in the lanes' coordinate space)
  -> VirtualObject: LaneBinding(F fields) + selection + N columns   (addresses only)
  -> BF16 tiles: rows = fields, K = selected identities, cols = B columns
  -> GEMM: C[F x N] FP32
```

| | 64×64 | 256×256 |
|---|---|---|
| Rows (demanded fields) | 64 | 256 |
| K (selected identities) | 64 → 2 steps of 32 | 256 → 8 steps of 32 |
| Output tiles 16×16 | 16 | 256 |
| K=32 dot-product steps (logical) | 32 | 2,048 |
| One BF16 operand | 8 KiB | 128 KiB |
| FP32 C | 16 KiB | 256 KiB |

At 256, the shape is 256 fields × 256 columns over 256 selected identities.
It is not an identity × identity adjacency matrix. The 256 fields bind
directly under `Extent::DR256_SQ_X64`, because DT64's `MAX_FIELDS` is 256, so
no composition of smaller bindings is needed and no production lane budget
changes. The lab source holds 256 lanes. That says nothing about which lanes
are resident in production (see #1973 §0).

**Logical tile geometry is not the physical operand.**
- A direct A tile is 16 consecutive slab rows at the slab pitch (65,536 bf16),
  offset by the region start.
- A staged A tile is a dense 16 × K buffer.
- B is N/16 VNNI-packed K × 16 column panels.

Run-to-run ranges over the three runs:

| Arm | 64×64 | 256×256 |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | 4.0–5.9 µs | 146–178 µs |
| Staged | 4.1–4.9 µs | 140–155 µs |
| Warm end-to-end | 6.7–7.2 µs | 170–197 µs |

The staged arm's one allocation is its scratch buffer on first use. A caller
that keeps the buffer, as the timed loop does, allocates nothing.

**Sparse demand** (256 fields × 256 columns, 40 scattered identities, mask
WHERE):
- logical selection: 40
- K padded to 64, so 512 dense dot-product steps: the padding is multiplied
- lanes borrowed: 256 (descriptors, not bytes)
- A bytes copied: 20,480 (only the selected values)
- staged AMX: 392–557 µs over five runs, slower than the dense 256-identity region. Each
  value is gathered by a bit walk; the arithmetic is not the cost.

A sparse selection does not give sparse arithmetic here. It compacts K, then
pays for the gather.

## Findings

1. **The shape routes; DT64 stores nothing.** At both sizes one binding with
   one check yields every lane, with no allocation. A direct read copies 0
   source bytes. Resolve borrows exactly one lane per field, whatever the
   universe size (tested at 256 and 65,536). No global G2L is used.
2. **Direct and staged are not separable by time on this host.** Copying A
   costs 8 KiB or 128 KiB, and at these sizes that is hidden in the tile
   loads; the two arms overlap within run-to-run noise. Direct's advantage
   is structural: zero copy, zero allocation, zero scratch. It is not a speed
   claim.
3. **The power-of-two pitch probably costs a little.** With a 65,568-wide
   pitch instead of 65,536, the direct read was 10–20% faster at 256 in every
   run. That is consistent with the 16 A rows (128 KiB apart) sharing L1
   sets. It is inside the noise band, so it is a hypothesis to test on
   dedicated silicon, not a finding.
4. **Preparing B is the price at 256.** Packing B costs about as much as the
   GEMM (149 µs vs 146 µs). Reusing the prepared B halves end-to-end time
   (cold 368 µs vs warm 170 µs). The reuse is keyed to the selection, the
   frame issue, the B revision and the width, and is refused when any of
   them changes. Tests show that using a stale B gives a wrong answer.
5. **Resolve with a check per field is quadratic.** `LaneBinding::lane`
   re-checks every bound lane on each call: 155 µs at 256 fields, against
   14 µs for one check plus borrows. At 256 the single check is still about
   8% of warm end-to-end time.
6. **Scaling 16 → 256 tiles is sensible.** MACs grow 64× (fields, columns
   and K each grow 4×). Direct time grew 25–44×, so throughput rose from
   about 45–70 to 95–130 GMAC/s. B preparation grew 11–20× for 16× the
   bytes. At 256, arithmetic and preparation are about equal, and the
   single check is the third cost.
7. **ndarray CBLAS BF16 is 7–11× slower than either tile arm.** It makes 263
   allocations and 785 KiB of temporaries at 256: it packs both operands and
   re-packs B per 16×16 tile. #1984 measured about 20× on its whole-lane
   workload.

## Refusals (all tested)

Each refusal is tested two-sided where a bypass is constructible, with the
wrong answer shown:
- **Direct:** a scattered mask (`NotARegion`); separately allocated lanes;
  permuted rows; a pitch without block padding.
- **Tails:**
  - At the end of the universe, tails of 63 and 65 read the slab's zeroed
    padding and are exact.
  - In the middle of the universe they are refused (`OverRead`). Without
    that refusal, a NaN in the next identity poisons the tile, even though
    B's padding row is zero.
- **Orientation:**
  - A square transposed slab declared in the identity row order reads wrong.
  - The same slab declared in its true row order is refused at bind.
- **Binding:** a cross-space selection; a stale frame; a foreign frame; the
  wrong reader scope; a changed field mapping; a different schema basis.
- **Prepared B:** a different selection, frame issue, B revision or width.

Disable runs: removing the over-read guard, the selection witness in the B
reuse key, or the binding check in resolve each fail the matching tests.

**Error policy.** BF16-exact integer fixtures must match exactly across all
four arms (accumulation stays below 2^24). For inputs rounded to bf16 on
purpose, the bounds are:
- between arms: `2 × K × 2^-24 × Σ|a·b|` (FP32 accumulation order);
- against the unrounded f64 answer: an additional `2^-8 × Σ|a·b|` (bf16
  round-to-nearest on A; B is exact).

## Resolve on `LaneBinding::borrow_all` (2026-11-10, after #1988)

`VirtualObject::resolve` now calls DT64's `LaneBinding::borrow_all`: one
binding check, then every bound lane in ascending field order, placed into the
view's row order. Any refusal, `Vanished` included, is returned before a lane
is placed, so no arm runs on a partially resolved object.

Lane provenance is now the identity of the object that resolved the lanes,
plus its frame issue. Both are compared in O(1).

The per-field `LaneBinding::lane` route is kept only as a benchmark
baseline.

Resolve, warm, medians of three runs each (raw output in `results/`):

| Implementation | 64 fields | 256 fields |
|---|---|---|
| #1987 as first measured: check, then `LaneBinding::addr` per field | 1.5–1.8 µs | 13.8–13.9 µs |
| `main` after #1987's lane-provenance fix (addresses stamped per row) | 3.9–4.2 µs | 26.3–28.0 µs |
| **`borrow_all` + object identity (this change)** | **0.97–1.2 µs** | **1.6–2.0 µs** |
| `LaneBinding::lane` per field (baseline) | 9.2–14.2 µs | 119–170 µs |

Why both earlier versions were slower:

- **#1987 as first measured was already quadratic.** `LaneBinding::addr`
  scans the bound lanes linearly, and the hand-written loop called it once per
  field.
- **The provenance fix made it worse.** It added a second `addr` per row in
  resolve, and a third in every arm's admission check.
- **`borrow_all` removes the scan.** It walks the bound lanes once, and the
  admission check compares one identity, so both are linear.

At 256 fields the check-once resolve is therefore about 7× faster than the
expected 14 µs, not merely preserved.

Warm end-to-end at 256 fields (resolve + direct AMX, B reused) fell from
190–199 µs on `main` to 104–108 µs. That figure includes the direct GEMM,
which varies run to run on this VM.

Correctness is unchanged:
- exact oracle parity for all four arms at both sizes;
- every refusal test passes;
- two new tests:
  - a lane mapped at bind but missing at resolve is refused as `Vanished`;
  - fields bound in reverse order give C's rows in reverse order.

Disable runs, each with the code restored afterwards:

| Disabled | Test that fails |
|---|---|
| Swallowing item refusals | the `Vanished` test |
| The object-identity check | `lanes_from_another_object_are_refused_by_every_arm` |
| Placing lanes in binding order instead of view order | the row-order test |

## D-DT64-GEMM-SHAPE-3: INT8 (u8 × i8 → i32) and the FACET STRIDE audit

`src/int8.rs` puts the same `VirtualObject` over u8 lanes and runs it as
`u8 × i8 → i32` through ndarray's INT8 facade: `backend::gemm_i8` →
`simd_int_ops::gemm_u8_i8` → AMX `TDPBUSD`. A stays unsigned and B signed,
which is TDPBUSD's native contract; there is no reinterpreting cast, and
`a_is_never_reinterpreted_as_signed` pins it (200 × −1 must give −200·K, not
+56·K). The INT8 and BF16 arms share one fixture. With `A_u8 = A + 8`, the
results satisfy `C_int8 = C_bf16 + 8·colsum(B)` exactly, and a test checks
this.

### A measurement correction to the results above

The earlier "B preparation" rows (149–181 µs at 256 fields) timed
`BOperand::value`, a hash computed per element, not data movement. The
benchmark now reads B from a `BTable` built once. Prepared VNNI B then costs
**46–62 µs** at 256 fields, about a third of the GEMM, not about equal to it.
Finding 4 above overstated preparation by about 3×.

### Results (256 fields, six runs on one host, `results/shape-2026-11-10-int8-run*.txt` holds the last three)

Same host, same fixture and same B table for every row. Ranges are across
runs. The VM's run-to-run variation is up to about 35%, so differences inside
that band are not findings.

| Step | BF16 | INT8 |
|---|---|---|
| Resolve (borrow_all) | 2.0–2.5 µs | 2.0–2.8 µs |
| Stage A | 0 B (direct) | 6–10 µs · 64 KiB (one copy per lane) |
| Stage A, per-value walk (baseline) | — | 36–56 µs |
| B | prepared once: 46–62 µs, then reused | in place for a region: 30 ns, 0 B; materialized: 58 µs |
| GEMM | 138–152 µs direct (110–122 GMAC/s) | 61–82 µs (204–275 GMAC/s), B VNNI-packed inside every call, 3 allocs · 12 KiB |
| of which: B band gather + VNNI pack, timed apart with ndarray's own `vnni_pack_i8` | — | 23–29 µs (36–38% of the GEMM; 54% at 64 fields) |
| Warm end-to-end | 103–160 µs | 66–101 µs |

At 64 fields, warm end-to-end is 6.5–6.7 µs (BF16) and 6.0–10.8 µs (INT8).

### FACET STRIDE audit

The six operations are separate, and each gets its own verdict.

| Operation | Where | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Positional source-lane reading | BF16 `direct` reads 16 slab rows in place at the slab pitch; INT8 must copy A because ndarray's INT8 facade takes dense A only | **REUSED** for BF16 (the pattern, not `simd_stride` code). INT8 stays a copy until ndarray gains a strided-A INT8 entry |
| 2. LUT transformation (`lut_u8`, `lut9`) | no byte transform anywhere in these pipelines; `simd_stride` has no GEMM callers in ndarray | **NOT APPLICABLE** |
| 3. Masked folding / reduction | GEMM arms do none; a scattered WHERE mask is compacted by a per-bit walk when A is staged | **NOT APPLICABLE** to the GEMM. The compaction is a candidate for a masking-ops compress; not measured here |
| 4. DT64 coordinate / shape routing | `borrow_all`, one check, O(N) | **REUSED.** The O(N²) `addr` walk is gone (see the borrow_all section) |
| 5. VNNI operand packing | `vnni_pack_bf16` / `vnni_pack_i8` are plain index moves, no shuffles | **NOT APPLICABLE:** required by the hardware. But INT8 re-packs B inside every call because there is no `PackedI8B`; that is repeated work, not a layout problem |
| 6. AMX dot-product accumulation | TDPBF16PS / TDPBUSD through the facade | **REUSED** |

**Prepared-B reuse key.** Reuse is keyed on authoritative identity, never on a
digest:
- the object that was packed for (one compare);
- for any other object, the selection compared in full, the frame issue and
  the width;
- the operand's identity: the defining function's seed, or the immutable
  table's construction instance, plus the revision.

The earlier FNV selection key is gone. Two tables with equal values and an
equal revision are different operands. Tests: `prepared_b_reuse_is_keyed_by_identity_not_a_digest`,
`prepared_b_is_reused_and_invalidated`.

**OPTIMIZATION FOUND** (measured, lab-local, no semantic change):
- **Copy A per lane.** A region of A is now one copy per lane instead of a
  per-value walk: 3.5–6× faster at 256 fields.
- **Borrow B in place.** For a full-width region, B comes straight from the
  table: 30 ns instead of 58 µs, and 0 bytes written.
- **Resolve with `borrow_all`.** Single-check resolution is 7× faster than
  #1987's original.

**Not proposed: an ndarray change.** A strided-A INT8 entry plus a prepacked
INT8 B, the INT8 twins of #414 and `PackedBf16B`, would save at most:
- the A copy, 6–10 µs, about 10% of INT8 end-to-end;
- the per-call B pack inside `gemm_i8`, which the facade does not expose and
  so was not measured on its own.

Measured: the per-call B band gather plus pack inside `gemm_i8` is 23–29 µs
at 256 fields, 36–38% of the INT8 GEMM, and the A copy is 5–7 µs. Together
that is about 30–45% of INT8 warm end-to-end time. This is an independently
measured case for a separate ndarray PR. It is proposed, not part of this
change.

**Central question.** DT64 is routing SoA positions, not moving them. Four
places paid for the same work twice, all found by measurement, and the first
three are now fixed:
1. per-field `addr` scans in resolve;
2. a per-value walk where a region copy would do;
3. hashing inside a "preparation" timer;
4. INT8 B re-packed on every call: measured at 36–38% of the INT8 GEMM, and
   open at the ndarray layer.

The FACET STRIDE guard (`facet-stride-guard.sh --diff`) reports 0 hits on
this change. ndarray is unchanged.
